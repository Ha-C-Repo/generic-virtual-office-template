Thesis: Claude Cowork can reliably automate construction admin and estimating prep only if you constrain it hard - small human-checked steps, a README map of the folder, your own rates and templates supplied as data, and reusable Skills - and you reserve high-stakes work (estimates, takeoffs) for AI to CHECK, never to generate.

- t=16:37-19:35 The human/behavioral problem (harder than the technical one). Automation bias: a polished, confident AI answer makes it almost impossible to go back and check it yourself; studies gave people faulty AI and they did WORSE than people with no AI because they stopped checking. Skill atrophy: over-reliance erodes the genuinely hard cognitive work (checking an estimate, thinking through methodology and build sequence) that lets you stand out in a commoditized sector. Errors of omission: hallucinations are easy to spot, but omissions are "almost impossible" to catch if you have not done the work yourself, and they are more common.
- t=19:35-21:49 The decision framework: three categories by "blast radius."
  - Low blast radius (e.g. populating project templates): fine to fully use AI; a jumbled project name "is not the end of the world."
  - Medium blast radius (important tasks you would skip when busy): e.g. a bid where the client sent 50 documents - you read the key ones and drawings yourself to ~95% confidence, then use AI to scan the 50 supplementary specs you are unsure are relevant. AI enhances your ability and reduces risk.
  - High blast radius (estimate, quantity takeoff): the only role of AI is to CHECK your finished work, never to draft or build it. Prepare the estimate yourself, then have AI run the same workflow independently and cross-check for missed items. "You should never be using it to generate stuff that has a high impact on project performance."

## The workflow, step by step (reproducible how-to)

1. Keep a standard TEMPLATE project folder (SWMS, ITPs, registers, quality docs, a document register, and a README.md).
2. Put a plain-text README.md in every working folder that maps the structure and tells the AI where to find and what to update (use placeholder tokens like [PROJECT NAME], [CONTRACT NUMBER], [SITE ADDRESS]). Mark each folder "AI reads these" vs "AI updates these." Let AI draft the README if needed.
3. Open Cowork inside that specific folder and grant file access (the Always allow / Allow dialog).
4. Issue ONE small, verifiable instruction at a time (one or two actions max). After each, read the output, check it against source, and iterate.
5. Always pass the workflow as: explicit data source(s) + required output format. Supply your own rates, production rates, and templates as files so the AI uses your numbers, not invented ones.
6. Codify any repeated workflow as a Skill (SKILL.md = standard operating procedure + a references/ folder full of real examples + optional scripts/). Skills auto-invoke on trigger phrases and keep the chat context lean versus mega-prompts.
7. Use the Progress todo panel to watch the discrete steps; confirm at each "human review" gate before the next step runs.

## What works / what does NOT (where he trusts AI vs refuses it)

- Trusts AI fully for LOW blast-radius template population and data reformatting (moving data between formats, populating registers from a defined source).
- Trusts AI for MEDIUM blast-radius supplementary scanning - reading the 50 "maybe relevant" specs after he has personally read the key documents and drawings to ~95% confidence.
- REFUSES to let AI generate HIGH blast-radius outputs: estimates and quantity takeoffs. AI's only allowed role there is to independently re-run and CHECK work he already did. Reason: compounding multi-step error (0.98^30 = 54%, 0.90^30 = 4%), automation bias, and undetectable errors of omission.
- Explicitly distrusts AI image analysis of construction drawings (ClockBench 50.6% vs human 90.7%) - the single most important refusal for any drawing-based takeoff.
- Distrusts "unconstrained reasoning": never lets it invent production rates or activity breakdowns.
