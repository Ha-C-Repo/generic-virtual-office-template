## Field Fixes - Part 6
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wBczYp9NRno | Duration: 37:14 | Upload: NA | Tier: 1 | Watched: 2026-06-24
### Core Concept
This webinar segment covers field fixes for connection problems: bolt and weld load sharing, mislocated or missing holes, gusset and bracing connection misfits, hole reaming and filling, weldability of steel, weld inspection, welding procedure specifications, and weld crack repair. The unifying theme is verifying a problem against the spec and the actual demand before forcing a costly repair, and detailing connections up front to absorb field variation. The speaker repeatedly contrasts the pre-2005 and 2005 AISC rules on bolts sharing load with welds [00:01].
### Spec References
The major cited change is the AISC 2005 specification rule that bolts shall not be considered to share load with welds, with the shear-connection exception that any permitted bolt grade in standard holes or short slots transverse to the load may share load with longitudinally loaded fillet welds, and that the bolt available strength in such connections is limited to 50% of bearing-type bolt strength [00:34][01:04]. Also cited: AWS D1.1 preheat tables and the AWS D1.1 annex for high-carbon steels [18:08]; ASTM A6 / A992 requiring the carbon equivalent on the mill cert [17:06]; the differing carbon-equivalent formulas between ASTM and AWS [17:38]; the 6G / 6GR welder certification for unbacked HSS CJP welds [22:45][23:15]; and AWS D1.1 Section 6.8, engineer-of-record approval for alternate acceptance criteria [33:30].
### Technical Details
The 50% load-sharing logic comes from University of Alberta research: slip-critical bolts will have slipped before welds deform enough to pick up load, but longitudinally loaded fillet welds have enough deformation capacity to combine with bearing bolts in standard holes; oversized holes force welding the full connection strength [01:35][02:06]. For a missing bolt row, reduce remaining bolts to 50% capacity and design the weld for the difference rather than welding only the missing-bolt portion [03:08][03:38]. The uniform force method allows alternate load paths: analyzing a moment on the gusset surface can remove the horizontal force from angle outstanding legs so the angles need only be welded for vertical shear [07:18][08:20]. The speaker notes A992 carbon equivalents typically land in the low 0.30s and are very weldable, while plate is the commodity item most likely to carry high carbon and govern preheat [18:39][19:09].
### Fabrication Notes
Preferred detailing replaces clip angles with an end plate or a continuous coped member to control the brace-connection dimension in the shop, even though SDS2 defaults to angles and the detailer must be told to change it manually [06:15][06:47]. Hole misfits are fixed by reaming (typically an eighth inch larger to the next bolt size, with edge distance rechecked), field welding, or filling and drilling as the least desirable option since AWS has no general hole-restoration procedure outside fatigue cases [08:52][09:23]. The shop rule is to run the same-size reamer through a couple of off holes and clean them if removal stays within a sixteenth to an eighth of an inch [11:26]. Weldability checks include the mill cert carbon equivalent, wet chemistry (last cost under $300), and the ironworker fillet-break test where a break through the weld is good and a break off the base metal indicates a carbon problem [19:39][20:10].
### Erection Notes
Short-slotted one-sided single-plate or single-angle connections absorb end-slope variation from camber, which can reach three-eighths of an inch [10:25]. Columns tied with precast plank historically walked out of plumb when the precast crew slammed planks in, so the fix is to provide steel tie beams (struts) at one per tier to hold column spacing and alignment, which is cheaper than arguing over plumb responsibility [13:32][14:33]. The erector determines what erection aids are needed but the fabricator details and furnishes them, so the two must coordinate, especially with architecturally exposed HSS where any aid must be cut, ground, and finished [16:05][16:35].
### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
Visual inspection is the primary method: no cracks, base-to-weld-metal fusion, filled craters and intermittent fillets, correct weld profile (slight crown, not concave), allowable weld size, undercut limits, and porosity limits [20:42][21:13]. Named failures include cracks from joint restraint, insufficient preheat, excess carbon, wrong procedure, wrong joint, moisture contamination, or out-of-spec material, illustrated by a plate that arrived as abrasion-resistant material with Brinell over 400 [29:21][30:23]. A Casa Grande moment-connection joint cracked three times from thermal stress on an end wall until the joint was redesigned [30:54][31:25]. Banging bolts traced to column settlement forcing bolts into bearing [32:26]. Post-Northridge inspectors found seismic demand-critical welds with passes two or three times the WPS maximum [25:15].
### Bid / Cost Implications
The recurring cost message is to weigh a field repair against the real demand rather than reflexively fixing. Changing the SDS2 default to an end plate saves shop labor but costs engineering time, an explicit trade-off [06:47]. The owner must specify NDT (mag particle, UT) in the contract documents; an owner who requires it after the fact owns all associated costs including rework unless gross negligence is proven [28:20][28:51]. AWS D1.1 Section 6.8 lets the engineer accept an undersize weld that still carries the shear, avoiding an unneeded repair pass; one example accepts a quarter-inch weld where a five-sixteenth was called out [33:30][34:33]. A fabricator keeps roughly a dozen WPS in a book that cover most structural welds, adding special ones as they arise [27:19].
