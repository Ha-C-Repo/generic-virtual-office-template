## Steel Bridge Fabrication
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FglCqdCewkY | Duration: 1:28:00 | Upload: NA | Tier: 1 | Watched: 2026-06-24
### Core Concept
This is an AISC live webinar (session L1, October 12 2017) on how steel plate girder and tub girder bridges are fabricated, presented by Dr. Karl Frank, retired chief engineer at Hirschfeld Industries [00:02]. The central message is that a steel bridge is a modular, custom-built product fitted in the shop so the field work is reduced to bolting splices with a wrench [02:05]. Girders are deep, thin-webbed, unsymmetrical composite plate sections custom-developed from a clean sheet rather than picked from a rolled-beam table, to produce a strong, ductile structure [03:39]. The talk walks the full shop sequence: order plate, splice and trim, cut camber, weld web-to-flange, fit stiffeners, splice, inspect, blast, paint, ship.

### Spec References
The speaker cites the AASHTO bridge design specification for girder proportioning limits (web slenderness near 120, flange b/t at 9.2 for Grade 50, flange thicker than web) [04:41][06:14][08:18]. Welding and inspection are governed by AWS D1.5, the bridge welding code, explicitly named, with preheat and interpass temperatures from D1.5 Section 1.5 and maximum amperage by process from Table 5.1 [25:24][28:30][74:41]. Narrow gap electroslag welding was adopted into D1.5 in 2010 after a federal moratorium was lifted in March 2000 [37:51]. Phased-array UT is noted as now recognized in D1.5 [65:48]. Heat input is measured in kilojoules per inch with each pass within 10 percent of the overall average [27:59].

### Technical Details
Plate girders are three plates (two flanges plus web) with stiffeners; tub (box) girders use the same web-to-flange welding but assemble two webs into a box with cross frames [02:37][54:22]. Span-to-depth ratio of 25 to 30 sets the starting depth; webs held under a 137 slenderness avoid longitudinal stiffeners; top flange runs about two-thirds the area of the bottom flange [05:11][16:03][07:16]. The compression-flange-to-tension-flange stiffness ratio limit matters because pushing toward a tee section voids the lateral torsional buckling equations [10:24]. Fusion welding melts a consumable electrode and base metal; rapid cooling in the heat affected zone can form hard martensite, which preheat is used to prevent by slowing the cooling rate [16:33][24:21]. Residual (locked-in) tensile stresses are unavoidable, are not calculated, and are accounted for in the specifications through the inelastic compression-flange curve [55:24][57:29].

### Fabrication Notes
Fabricators do not stock plate; they order on award, with Grade 50 at 4 to 8 weeks and 70W out to 10-plus weeks, plate length capped near 80 feet by rail cars [13:27][14:28]. Submerged arc is the most common process; flange butt welds bevel the plate, weld multiple passes one side with run-off tabs, then flip, air-arc gouge the root to sound metal, grind, and weld the back [33:44][36:17]. Welder qualification runs through a weld procedure specification backed by a PQR from tested plates (all-weld-metal tension, Charpy V-notch, side bends, macro etch, plus RT, and UT for narrow gap electroslag), good typically one to five years [26:28][30:04][31:06]. Flange thickness transitions should be made at field splices and the plates nested so several flanges are cut from one slabbed assembly, turning eight separate welds into two [45:35][47:08]. Quarter-inch stiffeners are disliked because the dual-arc stiffener welder burns through them [53:50].

### Erection Notes
Field assembly is mostly bolting splice plates with cranes and shoring towers; the shop guarantees fit so erection goes fast [02:05]. Heat curving for sweep is done after the flange is attached to the web but before stiffeners go on [82:11]. At most two curved girders are laid down together because stacking curved pieces climbs into the air [66:20]. Shipping drives design: girders up to about 12 feet tall ship upright on single permits; over 120 feet long needs special superload permits and added days; very deep girders may ship web-horizontal, and a stiffening truss can be added for lateral torsional buckling capacity in transit [73:39][74:11]. Rail is standard, with barge as a last resort, as on the Tappan Zee girders [73:05].

### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
The case study where an estimator assumed a 3/8 inch flange dimension was a misprint and bid 3/4 inch, then was forced to build the 3/8 inch flanges that failed the b/t and flange-thicker-than-web limits, ending in a bolted cover-plate retrofit, is the headline lesson: pay attention to a detailer RFI [10:55][12:26]. Visual inspection rejects non-uniform fillet welds; magnetic particle finds surface and near-surface cracks on fillets; radiography (gamma or x-ray, cobalt-60 above three inch plate) and ultrasonics find internal volumetric flaws, lack of fusion, and lack of penetration [57:29][59:04][61:10]. A flaw above the allowable threshold is gouged out, rewelded, and reinspected [81:06]. Adding bolts that are not needed (one splice shown had 312 bolts and 936 holes, another 634 bolts) drives field cost [67:22][68:24].

### Bid / Cost Implications
Material is bought only after award, so lead time (4 to 8 weeks Grade 50, longer for 70W) sits on the schedule front end and cannot be compressed [14:28]. Changing flange width within a field section rather than at a splice raises cost about 35 percent because the weld can no longer be slabbed [48:44]. HPS 70 thick plate behaves like gold: a 12 by 80 foot plate may be bought just to rip one 21 foot piece of 2.5 inch plate, so vary plate width across girders, not thickness, and extend plates to a common point for slabbing [51:17][52:18]. Virtual assembly (laser-tracker hole measurement plus computer-fit splice plates) eliminates hand drilling and multiple physical lay-downs, measuring to under a thousandth of an inch, which is the major speed and cost win [68:24][75:12].
