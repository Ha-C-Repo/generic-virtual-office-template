## Bolting & Welding Primer - Part 4

### Core Concept
This part of the primer is a weld-selection and weld-sizing lecture. It teaches how to choose among fillet, PJP groove, CJP groove, and plug or slot welds for the four basic joint types, how to choose weld-metal strength (matching, undermatching, overmatching), and how to size fillet welds against both strength demand and the prequalified minimum-size table. The throughline is that the efficient weld on paper is not always the cheapest weld in the shop, because joint preparation and cracking control drive real cost.

### Technical Details
A PJP groove gets more capacity per unit of weld metal than a fillet because its throat is the least dimension to the root, about 41 percent larger than the 45-degree fillet throat, so a same-strength PJP needs about half the weld metal [00:31][01:03]. The practical rule of thumb: if required throat is 3/4 inch or less, use fillet welds; above 3/4 inch, PJP usually saves money because the bevel-prep cost is offset [01:35]. Matching weld metal is required only for CJP groove welds loaded in tension; everywhere else, equal-to-or-less-than matching (undermatching) is permitted, and undermatching lowers and spreads residual stress for better crack resistance, called optimized weld metal in high-performance steel work [05:44][06:14][07:47]. Overmatching is never required and can be non-conservative because the fusion zone, not the smaller weld, becomes the weak link [08:17][08:49]. A 1/4 inch E70 fillet is good for 3,710 pounds per linear inch in shear along its throat; the design value is 30 percent of electrode classification times the 0.707 factor (about 21 KSI for E70) [20:39][21:10].
### Fabrication Notes
The prequalified minimum fillet size table governs over the calculated size in practice; multiple examples calculate 3/16 inch but the table forces 1/4 inch because of base-metal thickness [22:10][25:14]. The minimum-size note depends on hydrogen practice, which is a fabrication and cracking issue, not a load issue: for non-low-hydrogen processes the controlling thickness is the thicker part, for low-hydrogen it is the thinner part, and because A992 is welded low-hydrogen the table now keys off the thinner part [22:40][24:13][24:44]. Lamellar tearing comes from through-thickness shrinkage strains pulling steel inclusions apart, set off by specifying a CJP where a PJP would do; beveling the offending member and adding an inside fillet reduces it [12:56][13:59]. Do not fill misplaced or misaligned bolt holes with weld metal; set a bolt and tighten instead [04:10].
### Erection Notes
Erection-specific content is light. The main field-relevant caution is that welding inside a box section or box column typically meets the OSHA definition of a confined space and requires special precautions, plus the practical question of whether a person and equipment can safely get inside to make the inside weld [14:30][15:01]. Double-sided lap joints need both sides welded, or a plug/slot weld or internal mechanical support to prevent the root from straining and tearing under load when the far side cannot be reached [16:33][17:04].
### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
Named failures include lamellar tearing from over-specified CJP welds and through-thickness restraint [12:56], filling misaligned bolt holes with weld metal which has caused a variety of problems and is a poor load path [04:10], non-conservative results from deliberately overmatching weld metal because the fusion zone is not normally checked [08:49], and root tearing in single-sided lap joints loaded so the root is strained [17:04]. The speaker also flags using Blodgett's pre-1969 design allowables, which give over-conservative, oversized welds [20:08]. Plug and slot welds sit at the bottom of the fatigue allowable range and should not carry major cyclic load [03:40].
### Bid / Cost Implications
Weld choice is a direct cost lever: a PJP saves weld metal but adds bevel-prep labor, so the 3/4 inch throat rule of thumb decides which is cheaper to bid [01:03][01:35]. Because most structural fillets are small, fillets usually win on total cost since they need no joint prep [02:08]. The prequalified minimum-size table routinely drives the welded quantity above the strength-calculated size, so any weld-metal or labor estimate built only off demand will under-bid; the minimum-size jump from 3/16 to 1/4 inch is a recurring example [22:10][25:14]. Undermatching on higher-strength steel can reduce cracking rework, and over-specifying CJP where PJP suffices adds welding volume, prep, and lamellar-tear risk for no benefit [13:27].
