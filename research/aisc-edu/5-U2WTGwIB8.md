### Core Concept
This is a classroom escape-room style exercise teaching students how to navigate the AISC Steel Construction Manual efficiently to find code answers, with the framing that knowing where data lives in the Manual is the real skill. The lesson is procedural, not theoretical: use the pre-tabulated chapters and table of contents rather than randomly flipping pages [01:20]. It walks four worked lookups: axial compression strength of a column, economical beam selection, HSS width-to-thickness slenderness limits, and beam deflection, and emphasizes verifying an answer rather than guessing [02:22].
### Spec References
The exercise names specific Manual locations verbatim: Table 3-10 for beam design (the moment vs unbraced-length plots) [02:52], Chapter B and Table B4.1a for width-to-thickness ratios with the limit 1.4 times the square root of E over Fy [04:58], and Table 3-23 Case 7 for the simply supported beam deflection equation PL cubed over 48EI [05:54]. LRFD is the design basis stated for the column problem [00:50]. No AISC 341 seismic section, AWS D1.1 clause, RCSC provision, or numbered Design Guide is cited; this is a Manual-navigation drill, not a connection or welding lesson.
### Technical Details
Problem 1 finds the available axial compression strength by LRFD of a 14 ft long pinned W14x90 column with Fy equal to 50 ksi [00:50]. Problem 2 selects the most economical beam for an 18 ft braced length carrying a uniform distributed load of 26 kip per foot, simply supported, using Table 3-10 [02:22]. The instructor explains the dashed versus solid lines: plot the required moment against the unbraced length, then move up or to the right to the nearest solid line, which gives the lightest-weight-per-foot section that still works [03:25]. The class first proposes W18x130 (works but not economical) and corrects to W30x108, where 108 denotes 108 pounds per foot, lighter than the 114 figure mentioned [03:55]. Problem 3 finds the limiting width-to-thickness ratio (slender or nonslender) for a rectangular HSS via Chapter B and Table B4.1a [04:28]. Problem 4 computes maximum deflection of a center-loaded simply supported beam from Table 3-23 Case 7, PL cubed over 48EI [05:30].

### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
The named error is selecting a beam that satisfies the load but is not the most economical section: a student answers W18x130 when W30x108 is lighter and therefore the correct economical pick off Table 3-10 [03:25]. The instructor also flags the recurring confusion over dashed versus solid lines in Table 3-10 [03:25] and stresses verifying a computed answer rather than guessing at it [02:22]. A second mistake shown is random page-flipping instead of using the tabulated chapters and table of contents [01:20].
### Bid / Cost Implications
The most-economical-beam method maps directly to material cost: the lightest section that satisfies moment and unbraced length carries the least weight per foot, and structural steel prices by weight, so picking the correct solid line off Table 3-10 minimizes tonnage and dollars [03:25]. Choosing W30x108 over W18x130 is a direct weight, and therefore cost, reduction on every line of similar beams in a takeoff.
