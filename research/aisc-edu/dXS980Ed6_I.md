## SteelDay 2017: Designing in Steel
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dXS980Ed6_I | Duration: 59:23 | Upload: NA | Tier: 2 | Watched: 2026-06-24
### Core Concept
Larry Owner, AISC VP of engineering and research and a former fabricator at Douglas Steel, walks through what changed across the three documents that govern steel buildings: the 2016 Specification (AISC 360-16), the 2016 Code of Standard Practice (AISC 303-16), and the 15th edition Steel Construction Manual [00:31][01:03]. His framing point is the one fabricators most often get wrong: the Manual is a convenience, not law. The 2018 IBC references the Specification, and Chapter A of the Specification in turn references the Code of Standard Practice, so those two documents are the contract-binding ones, not the Manual [02:03][02:33].
### Spec References
Verbatim citations stated by the speaker: AISC 360-16 (the Specification) with Chapter A, Chapter D and Table D3.1 case 4 shear-lag revision, Chapter E (compression, KL replaced by L sub C), Chapter J connections (Sections J1.8 bolts-with-welds, J3 bolting, Table J3.3 hole sizes, J3.3 spacing/block-shear, J10 HSS now folded in from Chapter K), Chapter K retitled to additional HSS/box requirements, Appendix 1 advanced analysis, Appendix 3 fatigue, Appendix 6 member stability bracing, equation H1-1a [02:33 through 09:55, 41:38]. AISC 303-16 Code of Standard Practice, now an ANSI standard, Sections 3.1.1/3.1.2 (stiffening options), 1.10 (no zero tolerance default), 1.11 (protected-zone marking), 7.5.1 and 10 (anchor-rod and AESS tolerances) [15:43 through 30:44]. Referenced external standards: ASCE 7-2016, AWS D1.1-2015, RCSC-2014, ACI 318-14, ACI 117 (anchor-rod holes), ASTM A1065 and A1085 (HSS), ASTM F3125 umbrella bolt standard, ASTM F3043 and F3111 (200 ksi bolts), AWS A5.36 replacing A5.20/A5.29, ASTM F844 washers, ASTM A500 grade C, A572 grade 50 [04:39 through 46:18]. Manual tables named: 1-1, 1-15, 2-4, 2-5, 2-7, 6-2 (the super table), 7-14, 10-1, 10-14C, 14-2.
### Technical Details
Compression design drops KL for L sub C and eliminates the Q slender-element factor; the old K-factor alignment chart still lives in the Appendix 7 commentary if wanted [08:53][10:28]. The flagship Manual addition is the super table 6-2, which combines axial compression and biaxial bending capacity in one place; Owner worked a W14x99 at L sub C = 14 ft, reading 1130 kips compression, 642 kip-ft strong-axis and 311 kip-ft weak-axis, then ran H1-1a to an interaction of 0.928 [38:31 through 42:09]. A500 grade C is now the preferred HSS grade, raising rectangular/square yield to 50 ksi and round to 46 ksi, and 50 ksi is the preferred plate yield up to 4 inches thick [35:24 through 36:57]. Mill rolling-tolerance changes shifted the k, k1 and T detailing dimensions on many W-shapes (Table 1-1) [34:20].
### Fabrication Notes
The Code of Standard Practice now treats digital models as equal to drawings: design documents, fabrication documents and the fabrication model, approval documents, and erection documents replace the old shop/erection drawing terms, with model approval (EOR to detailer and back) recognized as standard practice [16:45 through 21:25]. Hole sizes grew: for one-inch-and-larger bolts the oversize allowance went from 1/16 to 1/8 inch per Table J3.3 because the larger F3125 bolts physically would not seat in the old holes [13:38][53:12]. Anchor-rod hole sizes in Table 14-2 all increased to match ACI 117 field-installation needs, and F844 washers are now permitted in place of fabricated plate washers within stated clearance limits, which removes a shop cost [45:48][46:18]. Washer requirements now point to RCSC rather than being duplicated in the Specification [15:11].
### Erection Notes
Erection coverage is light in this webinar. The relevant items are anchor-rod placement tolerances under Section 7.5.1, revised to align with the AISC Manual and ACI 117, and the protected-zone marking rule under Section 1.11: in high-seismic work the protected zones must be permanently marked (paint or sign) so that nobody welds or hangs attachments in them later once fireproofing hides the field marks [28:08][29:42][30:44].
### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
Owner names practical failure modes rather than rejection statistics. The big one is inconsistent bidding from vague drawings: a moment connection noted "continuity and doubler plates as required" let some bidders carry stiffening and others omit it, producing non-comparable bids, which Sections 3.1.1/3.1.2 now fix [22:28][22:59]. Misreading Section 1.10 as making unlisted tolerances zero-by-default is called out as a long-standing error [29:11]. He warns that the new 200 ksi F3043/F3111 bolts cannot be hot-dip galvanized and must not be used where corrosion is possible [54:49][55:20].
### Bid / Cost Implications
The stiffening-options change directly affects estimating fairness. Under option 3B the EOR who does not show required stiffening must give a bidding quantity, for example 70 percent of connections; if actuals come in lower the owner gets a credit and if higher the owner pays the difference, an equitable mechanism instead of guess-and-pad [23:31][24:33]. AESS now has five graded levels (AESS 1 basic, 2 viewed beyond 20 ft, 3 viewed within 20 ft, 4 showcase, C custom), and cost rises sharply with each step, so over-specifying AESS wastes money [26:34][27:36][28:08]. Preferred grades (A500 grade C, 50 ksi plate) carry no cost premium because mills already dual-certify and supply the stronger material [56:21][57:53]. F844 washers cut anchor-rod cost versus plate washers [46:18].
### Your Company Application
For Ivan's takeoff, the stiffening bidding-quantity rule under AISC 303-16 Section 3.1.1/3.1.2 is the actionable item: when a set says "doublers as required" without showing them, carry a stated stiffening percentage rather than zero or a silent allowance, which fits our verify-don't-generate posture and the confidence-tag flow. Ivan should also apply preferred grades (A500 grade C at 50 ksi, 50 ksi plate) since the material is already what mills ship, and should confirm availability of the very heavy W14/W36 and large HSS shapes before pricing them, because rolling once every six months can break a schedule. The model-equality language supports our Tekla detailing and in-house PE stamp workflow, where option 3 (fabricator's licensed engineer completes connection design) is exactly our model and the approval-document exchange describes our EOR review loop. Anchor-rod work maps to Table 14-2, ACI 117 holes, F844 washers, and Section 7.5.1 placement tolerance for our anchor design/setting.
