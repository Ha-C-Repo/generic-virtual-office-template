## Unified Design of Steel I-Section Flexural Members in the 2005 AISC and 2007 AASHTO Specifications
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4q7xXjnxBo8 | Duration: 1:23:20 | Upload: NA | Tier: 2 | Watched: 2026-06-24
### Core Concept
This is Don White's TR Higgins award lecture on the unified flexural strength provisions that were harmonized between the 2005 AISC Specification and the 2005/2007 AASHTO LRFD Bridge provisions [00:00][01:04]. The core idea is one consistent framework for the flexural strength of I-section members covering doubly and singly symmetric sections, compact, non-compact, and slender webs, hybrid webs and flanges, and non-prismatic geometry, built so the complicated cases stay tractable without making the simple cases harder [03:09][03:43]. The whole picture reduces to a single strength curve plotted against a slenderness term (lateral-torsional or flange-local), anchored by a plateau strength (Fmax / Mmax), an inelastic anchor point, and an elastic buckling branch [06:20][07:21]. This is research and code-development theory, not a fabrication or erection how-to.
### Spec References
The talk cites specification editions and provisions rather than fabrication clause numbers: AISC 2005 Specification and AISC 1999 (AISC 99) Provisions [18:43][19:16]; AISC ASD 89 (1989) for comparison curves [34:19][34:49]; AISC 2009 LRFD [33:46]; AASHTO 2004/2005/2007 LRFD bridge provisions [33:14][48:19]; AASHTO LRFD 1998 [72:05]; the SSRC Guide elastic-LTB equations [20:50][30:39]; and AISC Design Guide 25, design of frames using web-tapered members [44:42][47:18]. Parameters named aloud include RPC and RPT effective shape factors, RTS in the AISC manual, Cb moment-gradient modifier, and the slenderness limits Lambda-pw and Lambda-rw (HC/Tw of 137 as the non-compact web limit for Grade 50) [12:00][13:33][28:25]. No connection, bolting, or welding clause numbers (J-series, RCSC, AWS) are cited because the lecture is about member flexural strength.
### Technical Details
Strength is built from three segments: a plateau to anchor point 1 (the compact limit), an inelastic transition where flange stress reaches roughly 0.7 Fy (FL = 7/10 Fy) due to residual plus flexural stress, and the elastic LTB or flange-local-buckling branch [07:52][08:56]. The plateau Mmax is a function of web slenderness HC/Tw through the effective shape factors RPC and RPT, which equal one at the non-compact web limit and rise toward Mp/My at the compact limit [12:31][13:03]. For singly symmetric sections the speaker shows extreme cases where Mp can fall below My of the compression flange, so tension-flange yielding must be checked as a separate limit state [14:04][16:09]. Elastic LTB uses the rt (RTS) radius of gyration based on the compression flange plus one-third of the web depth in compression, with KY and KZ end-restraint factors set to one in the spec but allowed by the commentary, and Cb for moment gradient [22:53][23:24][27:00]. Shear post-buckling uses Basler tension-field action with a Vincent shear-buckling coefficient; moment-shear interaction was removed from AISC 2005 except where flanges are small (web-to-flange area ratio above 2.5 or web depth to flange width above 6), in which case post-buckling shear strength is disallowed [50:21][52:24][53:27]. Curved-girder and lateral-bending effects are handled by the AASHTO one-third rule, treating the flange as an equivalent beam-column [65:23][69:29].
### Fabrication Notes
The lecture does not address shop fabrication, fit-up, or QC. The only fabrication-adjacent points are member type distinctions that matter to a fabricator's catalog choices: the provisions make no strength distinction between rolled and welded shapes even though welded three-plate girder test data sit slightly lower [40:32][41:06], and hybrid built-up girders (different web and flange yield strengths) and web-tapered built-up members are explicitly covered [04:13][44:42]. A simple welder-warping detail is mentioned: boxing in the end of a beam creates warping restraint (KZ less than one) to raise LTB strength [21:52].
### Erection Notes
No erection sequencing, crane, or rigging content. The one field-relevant warning is on bracing: the bracing provisions are written to develop the K=1 LTB strength, so if a designer squeezes capacity by using K less than one for lateral-torsional buckling, the bracing requirements are not covered by code and lab tests have shown braces failing before the member [31:39][32:12]. There is also discussion of construction-stage loading on bridge girders (wind, eccentric overhang bracket loads, skew, horizontal curvature) that drives flange lateral bending before the deck is composite [05:15][68:28].
### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
No weld or bolt inspection failures are discussed. The errors named are design and code-application errors: applying the doubly symmetric RTS equation to a singly symmetric section, which does not work [23:56]; the AISC 99 anomaly where an extreme section checked as both compact and slender web simultaneously, resolved conservatively in 2005 by classifying it slender [18:43][19:16]; discretizing long members into many elements in software that lacks warping torsional stiffness, producing a fictitious flexural-torsional buckling that the analysis is not actually modeling [29:37][30:09]; and the false magnification of moment-shear interaction that results from evaluating moment at a shifted location for high shear but at the peak for low shear [62:46][63:17].
