## Moment Connections, Part 1

### Core Concept
This is AISC Live Webinar session 1 of 4 (Connection Design for Moment Frames and Braced Frames), taught by Dr. Brad Davis from slides originated by Dr. Thomas Murray. It covers fully restrained (FR) moment connections for wind and low-seismic frames, not high-seismic, so AISC 341 seismic detailing is largely out of scope here. The design model throughout is that the beam end moment is resolved into a flange force couple, the flanges carry the moment, and the single web plate carries only direct shear with no eccentricity [09:52][10:47]. Three connection families are taught: directly welded flange, flange-plated welded, and flange-plated bolted, followed by the six column-side limit states and a full worked example [01:58][32:48].

### Technical Details
The example is a two-sided directly welded flange connection: W24x103 beams (A992) at Mu = 882 kip-ft and Vu = 75 kips each side, into a W14x159 column (A992) with axial load 1200 kips above and 1335 kips below and a story shear of 90 kips [53:57]. The beam moment decomposes to a flange force of 450 kips (882 kip-ft / 23.5 in moment arm = beam depth minus flange thickness) at each of the four flange points [57:38]. Column flange local bending gave phi-Rn 398 kips, web local yielding 370 kips, web crippling 556 kips, so half-depth stiffeners are needed at the tension points and full-depth transverse stiffeners are required top and bottom [58:53][60:01]. Net stiffener forces came out 80 kips per pair (450 minus the governing column limit), and the panel-zone shear was Vu = 810 kips (450 + 450 minus 90) against a column web strength of only 259 kips after the high-axial reduction (alpha-Pr 1270 kips exceeds 0.4 Py 934 kips) [70:20][70:43].

### Fabrication Notes
The directly welded flange connection is shop-fabricated by welding the single shear plate to the column and beveling the beam flange ends with weld access holes; the CJP flange welds with backing are then made in the field downhand [04:53][05:25]. PJP welds are explicitly not recommended for directly welded flanges because filling a beveled flange from one side leaves a natural crack/stress riser at the root on the far side [82:43][16:14]. Fillet-weld sizing for flange plates uses the Manual shortcut 1.392 kips per sixteenth per inch (times 1.5 for transverse loading), and the spec J2 minimum fillet is 1/4 in, which governed the stiffener welds in the example [08:00][66:14]. Final example fabrication: two 1/2 x 6 A36 stiffener plates top and bottom with 1/4 in double-sided fillet welds, clipped 1-1/2 in at corners to clear the column fillet, plus two 3/4 in Gr.50 doubler plates one each side of the web [63:01][frame slide 79][frame slide 87].

### Erection Notes
The connection is designed to keep field welding downhand and avoid overhead welds, which is why the bottom flange plate is made wider than the beam flange so its welds run on the accessible top face, and the top plate is made narrower [11:29][84:30]. Overhead welding is defined as the welder getting under the joint and holding the electrode upward, which is undesirable, so connection geometry is chosen to avoid it [84:30]. Field sequence matters: the plate-to-column weld must be completed before the plate-to-beam welds to account for weld shrinkage [12:11]. The backing bar at the directly welded flange relaxes the beam-length erection tolerance at the flange-to-column gap [05:31]. Flange-plated bolted connections set the top flange plate 3/8 in high above tabulated beam depth to absorb beam-depth and out-of-square mill tolerances (Manual Table 1-22) [24:24].

### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
PJP on a directly welded flange is called out as a defect-prone choice because of the embedded root crack, and a single-sided fillet on a directly welded cantilever flange has the same problem [82:43][83:55]. Flange holes in a bolted flange-plated connection trigger reduced flexural rupture (Spec F13.1) and can prevent developing beam Mp, so when the EOR requires the connection to develop the full beam capacity a holed flange-plated detail will not pass [29:36]. The bolt shear-transfer limit state is commonly misread: the strength is the sum over all bolts of each bolt's effective (minimum-of-five) strength, not the single weakest bolt applied to all (the older conservative poison-bolt method) [86:36]. Panel-zone shear is the classic counterexample where shear, normally non-controlling, governs and gets hammered by the flange force couples [47:00].

### Bid / Cost Implications
Doubler plates and transverse stiffeners are explicitly described as expensive and undesirable, so the speaker steers design toward column sizes that avoid them [50:52]. In the worked example a moderately sized W14x159 column under a fairly ordinary 882 kip-ft moment still required both full-depth stiffeners and two 3/4 in doubler plates, which is real added shop labor and plate that an estimate must carry. The recurring fabrication driver is avoiding overhead field welds, since downhand welding is faster and cheaper, which shapes plate widths and shop-versus-field weld choices [84:30]. Where a small cantilever or stub can be shop welded, fillet welds replace field CJP welds and cut cost [06:32].
