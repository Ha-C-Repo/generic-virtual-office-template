### Technical Details
Stability bracing is governed by two Yura equations, one for required cross-frame stiffness and one for required strength expressed as a moment demand, both driven by the factored girder design moment M sub F, span L, unbraced length (cross-frame spacing), girder depth h sub o, and girder stiffness [05:36][06:06]. Provided cross-frame stiffness is the inverse sum of three flexibilities: system stiffness beta-e (conservatively based on two girders), web distortional stiffness beta-sec (taken as infinity for a full-depth cross frame), and in-plane girder stiffness beta-g [12:49][13:51][14:53]. The worked example gave M sub F of 77,000 kip-in (Strength I controlling), required stiffness 225,000 kip-in/rad, provided 272,000 kip-in/rad, so the brace passed [19:59]. Part two ties wind pressure to a spacing-to-depth (s/D) ratio: at s/D less than 3 heavy shielding lets you apply CP 2.2 to the projected superstructure height, while s/D greater than 3 loses shielding and loads interior girders [31:24][36:28].

### Erection Notes
The central erection concern is that bare girders standing on substructure before the deck hardens are braced only at discrete cross-frame points, and wind on this partially constructed system can cause large lateral deflection and sway [02:34][38:59]. The presenter showed video of a long-span erection where cantilevers and mid-spans visibly flexed under roughly 50 mph wind, motivating deflection criteria such as L/150 [38:59][39:32]. Wind estimates also size the temporary restraints needed before anchor bolts are set and govern loads handed to the specialty engineer for falsework design [38:29][52:59]. Florida applies a 0.6 velocity modifier for construction periods under a year and a reduced 20 mph case when the contractor is actively on site and can watch the weather [45:12][45:43].
### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
The speaker noted recent steel I-girder bridge construction problems traced back to inadequate cross-frame and diaphragm bracing, which is why the stiffness and strength check matters [23:37]. A flagged design error is over-conservatism, using the maximum negative moment directly over the pier instead of moving out to the first cross-frame line where bearings and anchor bolts provide restraint [11:46]. A second flagged mistake is combining maximum factored construction dead and live load with maximum factored wind, since a deck is not cast during a windstorm [10:14].
### Bid / Cost Implications
The estimating takeaway is that including stability bracing forces on top of wind raised cross-frame design loads only modestly, and the example resolved to routine 5x5x7/16 angles for chords and diagonals, so the check rarely drives steel weight up [21:31][22:02]. The cost warning is on the temporary side: under-shielding or over-estimating wind beefs up falsework members and, more expensively, the temporary foundations under that falsework grow large and costly, so the goal is a reasonable system-level wind estimate, not a padded per-girder number [52:59].
