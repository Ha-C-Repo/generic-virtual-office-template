## Analysis and Design of Chevron Brace Connections with Flat Bar Gussets: Part 1
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7NqcCokyZWQ | Duration: 1:32:45 | Upload: NA | Tier: 1 | Watched: 2026-06-24

### Core Concept
Patrick J. Fortney (CSD Engineering / SCI Steel) teaches the analysis and design of Chevron (mid-span) brace connections using single gussets, with Part 1 limited to low-seismic and wind applications where AISC 341 strength and detailing do not govern [04:05][56:58]. The controlling idea is that a flat bar gusset places the centroid of the gusset-to-beam interface directly under the point where the brace line of action crosses the flange, so the interface carries only normal and shear forces with no moment, while a shaped single gusset shifts that centroid off the work line and introduces a moment that must be handled [11:18][14:26][34:04]. The method walks a full set of limit-state checks on the brace, the gusset, the welds, and the beam, then builds beam shear and moment diagrams from the interface forces treated as externally applied loads [37:45][55:27].

### Technical Details
The flat bar moment-free condition gives V1 L1 = H1 eB so the interface sees only the brace horizontal and vertical components, where eB is half the beam depth [33:32]. For shaped gussets the off-centroid distance Delta produces Ma = H1 eB - V1 L1 - Delta1 and Mb = V2 L2 - Delta2 - H2 eB, converted to an equivalent normal force N = 4M/x for weld sizing [34:34][36:09]. Sign convention: tension brace components positive, compression negative [23:45][24:15]. Buckling uses K = 0.70 for a gusset that is not at a member end, r = tg/root(12), and K = 1.0 with the Dowswell 2006 (EJ) approach for whitmore checks [31:57][47:39]. The worked example used HSS A500B braces, A992 Gr.50 wide-flange beam, and Gr.50 (A572-250) plate, with brace 1 at 118 kips tension on a 9.78:12 bevel (Theta 39.5 deg, components 91.1 and 75.1 kips) and brace 2 at 163 kips compression (components -126 and -104) [56:58][59:32][60:03].

### Fabrication Notes
Material guidance: flat bars are commonly A572 Gr.50, also A36 and A529 Gr.50, generally up to 12 in wide and 2 in thick, with width increments of 1/4 in under 3 in, 1/2 in from 3 to 6 in, 1 in from 6 to 12 in, and thickness increments of 1/8 in up to 1 in and 1/4 in above [12:21][13:23]. The key fabrication distinction is that a flat bar has sheared longitudinal edges while plate (shaped) material can be flame cut on all four sides [14:58]. The brace is slotted over the gusset; the slot is sized at gusset thickness plus 1/8 in, and the speaker uses nominal (not 0.93 design) HSS wall thickness when computing net area to allow for hand flame-cutting, grinding of notches, and undercut [40:55][65:50][78:29]. Choose bar width as brace width plus 1 in so a single-pass weld can fill, and target single-pass fillet welds rather than multi-pass to ease shop and field work [27:50][30:54].

### Erection Notes
The slot tolerance is an erection concern: too tight and the erector cannot slide the brace over the gusset, too loose and more weld is needed to bridge the gap [40:55]. In the field the slot can end up bearing on one face of the gusset with the full gap on the other side, or the erector may take time to center it; this is unpredictable, so the detail gives the welder the design weld and relies on the qualified welder to upsize for actual gaps [77:29]. The two-in clear distance from the brace leading corner to the flange is set partly for workmanship, inspection access, and erection fit-up [28:50]. No crane, rigging, or sequencing detail beyond fit-up is covered.

### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
The speaker flags upsizing the brace-to-gusset weld for the slot gap as a frequent miss; the design weld must be increased in the field to account for the gap, which is the welder's responsibility once given the design value [76:58][77:29]. He notes that omitting the panel-zone shear check on a tension-compression brace pair is an error and agreed it should be checked for single-gusset Chevron connections [80:32]. Two-story X frames are not directly addressed by the AISC 341 V and inverted-V provisions and must be treated carefully; in SCBF the beam must be continuous and the braces discontinuous through the joint [88:44][89:16]. Fouling of adjacent flat bars (x1r + x2r must be >= 0) is a geometry failure to guard against when coding the layout [20:38][29:21].

### Bid / Cost Implications
Economy drives the flat bar versus shaped gusset choice: flat bars use less plate and shorter weld and avoid the interface moment, so they are cheaper at small brace forces, but at high force the short interface forces a larger or multi-pass weld that can erase the saving [09:46][14:26]. The speaker repeatedly favors lengthening the connection to keep a single-pass weld over keeping it short and asking for multi-pass, an explicit labor-cost trade [30:54]. Shaped gussets cost more detailing time up front but cut back-end analysis effort and reduce beam eccentricity, which he judges worth it [18:37][19:07]. Net-area conservatism (nominal wall, slot +1/8) is a deliberate safety margin, not a code requirement, and a reviewer should not mark up the compliant design-thickness alternative [78:59].
