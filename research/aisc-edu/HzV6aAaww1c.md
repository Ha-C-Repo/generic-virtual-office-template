## Design for Stability Using the 2010 AISC Specification
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HzV6aAaww1c | Duration: 1:27:18 | Upload: NA | Tier: 2 | Watched: 2026-06-24
### Core Concept
Louis Geschwindner (former AISC VP of Engineering and Research) teaches the AISC 360-10 stability framework: how to determine the second-order required strengths PR and MR for beam-columns and then check them against available strength using the Chapter H interaction equations [01:36][02:37]. The structure of the spec is that Section B3.5 sends you to Chapter C, where C1 lists the five effects that must be considered, C2 tells you how to get required strength, and C3 tells you how to get available strength, and the method you pick for required strength dictates the method for available strength [06:15][17:36]. The three permitted methods are the direct analysis method (C2/C1.1), the effective length method (Appendix 7.2), and the first-order analysis method, with the direct analysis method as the foundation because it sets the applicability limits on the others [09:51][29:30][62:00].

### Technical Details
The interaction equations are required-over-available in force-and-moment form, unchanged from the 2005 spec [01:36]. Five effects must be considered per C1: member deformations (flexural, shear, axial), second-order P-big-delta sway and P-little-delta member effects, geometric imperfections, stiffness reduction from inelasticity and residual stress, and uncertainty in stiffness and strength [07:15][08:19]. Out-of-straightness is L/1000 (ASTM A6, already in the strength curves) and out-of-plumbness is L/500 (Code of Standard Practice) [24:22]. The direct analysis method reduces all stiffnesses to 0.8 and applies tau-sub-b only to flexural stiffness, which earns K=1 in design [40:18][41:20]. Loads are run at LRFD level or 1.6 times ASD (alpha = 1.6) so the higher second-order amplification is captured [08:19][34:05]. Notional loads of 0.002-alpha times the gravity load at each level model out-of-plumbness, and B2 thresholds (1.7 with reduced stiffness, 1.5 with nominal) decide whether the direct analysis method is mandatory [38:18][55:49].
### Fabrication Notes
This webinar is design-office analysis, not shop work. The only fabrication-adjacent content is the tolerance basis: column out-of-straightness L/1000 is an ASTM A6 mill tolerance already baked into the column strength curves [24:22]. No fit-up, cut, weld, or shop QC procedures are discussed.
### Erection Notes
The single erection-relevant fact is that the Code of Standard Practice permits a column to be erected out of plumb by L/500, and the stability methods explicitly account for that erected out-of-plumbness through notional loads or direct geometric modeling [24:22][31:31][38:18]. No sequencing, bracing-plan, crane, or rigging guidance is given; Appendix 6 bracing is referenced only to say you model real bracing in the analysis rather than applying the appendix forces [41:51].
### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
No shop or field inspection failures are covered. The errors named are analysis mistakes: failing to include the right deformations or mis-modeling supports (modeling fixed supports as pins ran one example deflection from 1.79 in to 7.11 in) [12:29][12:59], improperly omitting second-order effects, which the speaker says many engineers were doing [28:28], and the speaker's own live error of applying B1 to a no-translation moment that did not exist, corrected on air [81:04][82:06]. He also warns against faking stiffness reduction with a reduced modulus E, because that corrupts width-to-thickness checks on the design side [86:12].
### Bid / Cost Implications
No direct estimating, schedule, or pricing content. The indirect implication is that the analysis method chosen drives member sizes and therefore tonnage: leaning (gravity-only) columns design at K=1 and are cheap to handle, but in the example 50 percent of the columns carried 64 percent of the load, which raised the moment-frame column K from 1.6 to 2.67 and grew those members [59:24][63:33][64:34]. The direct analysis method, by eliminating the K calculation, removes a source of conservatism and rework on irregular frames [55:19].
