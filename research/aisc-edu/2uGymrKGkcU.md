## Understanding the Code of Standard Practice

### Core Concept
This is a 2014 NASCC Steel Conference panel, co-presented with CASE (Council of American Structural Engineers), walking through the AISC Code of Standard Practice (AISC 303) and the changes introduced in the 2010 edition [00:00][22:33]. The Code sets the trade customs and the division of responsibility among the owner's designated representative for design, the fabricator, and the erector, and it defines what must appear in the contract documents so each party can bid and build without guessing. The slide deck title throughout is "2010 Code Changes" and "Code Discussion Topics," organized by the Code's own section numbering. No audio transcript was available for this entry, so every citation below comes from the on-screen slide text.

### Spec References
All references are to the AISC Code of Standard Practice (2010 edition) as shown on the slides: Section 1.9 Commentary on unspecified tolerances [07:31]; Section 3.1 Structural Design Drawings and Specifications and its Commentary, listing required design-document content (a) through (g) and Section 3.1.1 through 3.1.6 [11:16][15:02][18:48]; Section 3.1.2 connection-design options (1) complete design shown, (2) selected/completed by an experienced steel detailer, (3) designed by a licensed PE working for the fabricator [22:33][30:04]; Section 3.1.2 continuation on service-load vs factored-load and LRFD vs ASD, and the PE-of-record written review of substantiating connection information [33:50][37:35]; Section 4.4 and its Commentary on the 14-day approval allotment [45:06]; Section 6.4 Fabrication Tolerances, specifically 6.4.4 camber [52:37][1:00:08]; and Section 7.10 Temporary Support of Structural Steel Frames, specifically 7.10.1 and its Commentary [1:03:54][1:07:39]. The deck also references the AISC Steel Construction Manual as the default source of connection tables when the design drawings provide none [30:04].

### Technical Details
Section 3.1 requires the design documents to state the size, section, material grade and location of all members, all geometry and working points necessary for layout, floor elevations, column centers and offsets, camber requirements, and the joining requirements between elements of built-up members [15:02]. Section 3.1.2 forces the owner's designated representative for design to pick one of three connection paths per connection, which determines who carries the connection-design liability [22:33]. A slide reproduces Figure 1-1 illustrating the column panel zone with column web stiffeners, the kind of detail the design must define rather than leave to the detailer [26:18]. When the detailer completes connections under option (2), the Code says the detailer shall utilize tables or schematic information in the design drawings, and absent that, tables in the AISC Steel Construction Manual or other reference information approved by the owner's representative [30:04].

### Fabrication Notes
Section 6.4.4 sets the camber tolerance and is the most concrete fabrication number in the deck [52:37]. A cambered beam received by the fabricator already holding 75 percent of the specified camber needs no further cambering. Otherwise, for beams 50 ft or less the camber variation is minus zero / plus one-half inch, and for beams longer than 50 ft the allowance is minus zero / plus one-half inch plus an additional one-eighth inch for each 10 ft (or fraction) over 50 ft. The second paragraph of 6.4.4 requires camber to be measured in the fabricator's shop in the unstressed condition for inspection purposes [1:00:08]. A cold-cambering detail slide shows a 45 ft beam with a 24 ft cambered middle zone and the corresponding moment diagram, illustrating that camber is induced over the central span, not end to end [56:23].

### Erection Notes
Section 7.10.1 makes the owner's designated representative for design responsible for identifying in the contract documents both the lateral-load-resisting system and the connecting diaphragm elements that give the completed structure its lateral strength and stability, and any special erection conditions required by the design concept, such as shores, jacks, or loads that must be adjusted as erection progresses to set or maintain camber, hold position within tolerance, or prestress [1:03:54]. The 7.10.1 Commentary explains that 7.10.1(b) covers special requirements inherent in the design concept that the erector could not otherwise know, for example sequentially erected structures where a member must reach a specific elevation in a later step; these must be disclosed so the erector can properly bid, plan, and perform the erection [1:07:39].

### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
The recurring failure mode the panel targets is incomplete or ambiguous contract documents: a tolerance the design concept needs but never specifies (the Section 1.9 Commentary point that the necessary tolerance should be written into the contract documents) [07:31], connections left undesignated so liability is unclear, and special erection or shoring requirements never disclosed to the erector [1:07:39]. No weld-profile accept/reject photos, NDE rejection criteria, or bolting inspection failures appear in this deck; this video is a document-and-responsibility code talk, not a QC defect catalog.

### Bid / Cost Implications
Section 4.4 Commentary states the 14-day allotment for return of shop and erection drawings represents the fabricator's portal-to-portal time, and that in the absence of contrary information in the contract documents, 14 days may be assumed for bidding, contracting, and scheduling [45:06]. When substantiating connection information is part of the submittals, a longer, modified allotment should be written into the contract documents. The panel's "Bubba Says" interludes drive the bidding point home: a schedule no one has actually agreed to is not a schedule, and changing one requirement ripples into what else it affects [03:46][48:52]. The three connection options in 3.1.2 also shift cost and risk, since fabricator-PE-designed connections (option 3) move engineering effort and liability onto the fabricator's price [22:33].
