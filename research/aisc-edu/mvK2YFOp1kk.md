### Core Concept
This segment teaches the mechanics behind the AISC bolt-design rules for bolts loaded in tension, then transitions into connected-material limit states (bearing, tear-out, block shear) and bolt-installation methods. The instructor derives why an externally applied tension load adds only a small increment to a pretensioned bolt, why the nominal tensile stress provision uses a 0.75 factor on the area rather than a strength reduction, and how installation torque is resisted by friction and disappears at ultimate so the bolt can be designed on pure tensile fracture [00:00] to [12:26].

### Technical Details
For a pretensioned bolt with external tension applied, the final bolt force equals the initial pretension plus a fraction of the applied external load, derived by adding a deformation-compatibility equation (PL/AE for bolt and plate) to the single available equilibrium equation; using a plate-to-bolt area ratio of about nine gives roughly a 10% increase, while physical tests show 7 to 8%, small enough to bury in the design expression [03:07] to [09:54]. The tensile stress area is about 0.75 of pi d squared over 4 because the threaded section governs, which is the true origin of the 0.75 factor [10:54] to [12:26]. Installation torque introduces torsional stress that is resisted by friction at the faying surface; as the plates separate that torsion incrementally vanishes, so at fracture the bolt is in pure tension [01:33] to [03:07].
### Fabrication Notes
The shop-relevant content is bolt installation control. Bearing-type connections that are not slip-critical may be installed to snug-tight only, defined as the ordinary effort of a worker on a spud wrench or the first impact of an impact wrench [23:13] to [23:43]. The speaker states a torque-to-pretension formula is too variable (off by about 40%) and is therefore not permitted as a calculated install method [24:15] to [24:45]. Calibrated-wrench installation uses a Skidmore-Wilhelm hydraulic load cell to set the wrench to stall at 1.05 times the minimum required pretension, requires a minimum of three bolts per calibration, must be re-run whenever lot, length, or diameter changes (a 3-inch versus 4-inch A325 of the same diameter needs separate calibration), and must be recalibrated at least once per day, with washers used to set a baseline friction condition [24:45] to [26:50].

### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
The recurring failure category named is bolts in tension combined with prying action and sometimes fatigue, which the speaker calls where most real bolted-connection problems fall [00:00] to [00:30]. He advises avoiding joints that put bolts in tension and preferring bolts in shear because shear fatigue life is far longer, and prefers A325 over A490 in tension for greater ductility [00:30] to [01:01]. Test specimens are used to show that block shear at ultimate is shear fracture across a small ligament plus tension fracture through the holes, not the pulled-apart appearance seen after the test, which is the basis for a corrected block-shear rule [18:08] to [23:13].
