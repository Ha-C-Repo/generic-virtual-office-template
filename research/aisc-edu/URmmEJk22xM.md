## Facade Attachments for Steel Framed Buildings
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=URmmEJk22xM | Duration: 1:27:58 | Upload: NA | Tier: 2 | Watched: 2026-06-24
### Core Concept
This webinar by Alec Zimmer of Simpson Gumpertz and Heger condenses AISC Design Guide 22, Facade Attachments to Steel Framed Buildings, which he co-developed with James Parker [00:32][02:36]. The central message is that the structural engineer of record (SER) owns the strategy for how the facade attaches to the building frame even though the facade and its attachments are usually delegated to a specialty structural engineer working for the contractor [05:11][37:05]. The talk works through facade performance fundamentals, design criteria, roles and responsibilities, accumulated tolerances and clearances, slab edge design, spandrel beam torsion, and masonry cavity walls, all centered on how the chosen attachment strategy drives the slab edge and spandrel beam design [04:08][82:07].
### Spec References
The speaker cites AISC Design Guide 22 (the subject document) [02:36], AISC Design Guide 9 for torsion of members rotating about their center [62:57][66:01], and the AISC Code of Standard Practice for the SER and specialty engineer definitions and for column out-of-plumb tolerances (2 inches in / 1 inch out at the tenth story in his example) [36:01][43:43]. For loads he cites ASCE 7 component-and-cladding provisions including the 0.7 reduction factor on wind for serviceability deflection [18:35], the seismic component equation with Rp and ap factors (connection designed at ap 1.5 / Rp 1.0, panel at ap 1.0 / Rp 2.5, so connections carry more than three times the panel force) [22:42][23:14], ASCE 7 Section 13.1.3 exempting seismic design category B (and SDC C under straight IBC) from facade attachment requirements [20:38][21:10], and the friction-clip prohibition for facade anchorage [21:41]. He also cites ACI 530 brick crack-control limits [28:52], the PCI Design Handbook 6th edition panel tolerances (plus/minus 0.5 inch, bow L/360) [44:15], the Steel Deck Institute pour-stop selection guide [52:05], and ASTM A615 / A706 deformed bar over welded wire fabric in slabs [51:35].
### Technical Details
Connection and system types covered: masonry brick veneer with light-gauge or CMU backup, relieving/shelf angles on hangers, story-tall and column-supported precast concrete panels, and aluminum-and-glass curtain wall with mullions [38:38][41:11]. Facade gravity load is eccentric to the spandrel beam and produces a horizontal component plus torsion; negative wind pressure combines with gravity eccentricity and often controls the attachment [15:59][19:37]. Spandrel torsion is resolved by kickers (double or single angles, or a channel bracing the bottom flange to the next interior beam), by roll beams (a wide-flange on a full-depth stiffener plate), or by plating the back of the wide flange to make a closed tube at atriums and stairwells where kickers cannot fit [54:12][67:33]. Two slab edge approaches: the slab cantilevers past the spandrel as a beam (needs top flexural reinforcement, loads distributed on a 1:1 slope), or the spandrel carries the eccentric load in torsion [48:27][50:32]. Accumulated tolerance is the aggregate of material production, fabrication/assembly, and erection/installation tolerances; his worked example yields a midspan gap range of plus 4.33 to minus 3.33 inches [42:42][45:22].
### Fabrication Notes
Pour stops at exterior slab edges are frequently beyond the 12-inch SDI table limit, so DG22 adds a bent-plate pour-stop table for extensions up to 18 inches from the spandrel beam, and a bent plate is preferred over light-gauge so it can be welded readily [52:05][52:39]. Forces from the vertical slab edge are transferred back into the slab with welded headed studs or deformed bar anchors on the pour stop vertical leg, and headed studs on a spandrel are usually field-applied because shop-applied studs are a tripping hazard during erection unless the bent plate follows after framing [52:39][53:11]. Relieving and shelf angles are always galvanized because water that gets behind the barrier collects on them and rusts them out [79:58]. Slabs use A615 or A706 deformed bar rather than welded wire fabric, which sinks to the deck and does no good in negative moment [51:35].
### Erection Notes
Field fit-up adjustability is built into the hanger detail with vertical slotted holes in the stiffener plate and horizontal slotted holes in the angle, set with erection bolts and then field-welded once the final position is fixed just before the brick goes up [78:22][78:55]. The shelf-angle-to-hanger connection uses a vertical long-slotted hole and an erection bolt, then a field weld at the top of the shelf angle, and the erection bolt is removed so it does not interfere with flashing or waterproofing [78:55][79:27]. Clearances are deliberately provided to absorb the accumulated tolerances and give access during erection, and the speaker notes the cost trade between long slotted holes and a field-welded connection in lieu of bolted [33:26][46:24]. Steel erectors often prefer a slotted hole at the roll-beam-to-spandrel connection, which is acceptable if the faying surfaces are prepared for slip-critical friction and the bolts are pretensioned to resist the moment [55:15].
### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
The recurring field failure is sealant bulging out of soft joints because the joint was sized too small and the brick grew with moisture and temperature [28:52][72:11]. Transitional masonry buildings (late 1800s to mid 1900s) built integrally with the frame crack and bulge as trapped moisture rusts the unprotected steel and the rust expands [08:15][09:16]. Corrugated brick ties are no longer used because they trap water on thin steel [34:27]. A historical defect he has seen is omitting the horizontal soft joint under the shelf angle (a 1962/1966 building), where the brick below jacked up the relieving angle and lifted the parapet [86:16][86:46]. A coordination error is the panel designer and the SER assuming different hinge locations, which mislocates the design moment [17:33]. Friction clips for facade anchorage are not permitted under seismic provisions [21:41].
### Bid / Cost Implications
Slab edge details are finicky and carry a lot of cost, and minor architectural or structural differences strongly affect constructability, so they must be addressed early [46:54]. Keeping perimeter conditions uniform and using similar spandrel sizes throughout makes everyone's life easier and cheaper, and the speaker explicitly warns that a net tonnage saving may not be worth the headache of supporting the facade at two dozen different spandrel beam types [48:27][59:19]. A wind tunnel study on a large project often shows pressures below ASCE 7, reducing cladding cost and main wind-force-resisting-system force [18:35]. Field-welded connections and long slotted holes add adjustability but cost more than plain bolted ones, a trade to price per project and region [34:58][46:24].
