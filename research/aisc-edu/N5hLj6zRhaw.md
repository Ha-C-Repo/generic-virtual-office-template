## Modules for Learning Structural Stability

### Core Concept
This is session three of AISC Night School "Stability Design of Steel Structures," presented by Dr. Ronald Ziemian of Bucknell (chair of AISC TC10 on frame stability) on February 9, 2015 [00:02]. The subject is nonlinear computational analysis as a "virtual laboratory" for understanding structural stability, taught through nine free downloadable learning modules paired with the free MASTAN2 software [03:35][22:27]. The driving idea is that limit-states design targets ultimate strength, where yielding and second-order effects combine, so the behavior lends itself to nonlinear analysis rather than closed-form differential equations [02:05][02:35]. This is a teaching and analysis-method webinar, not a fabrication or connection-design lecture; it does not cover shop or field work.

### Technical Details
The modules walk through elastic column buckling versus end restraint (K = 0.5 to 2.0 reproduced from pi-squared-EI over (KL)-squared back-solved from the buckling load) [39:43][40:14]; flexural buckling column curves with imperfections and residual stresses on or off, compared to the AISC curve [42:15][43:16]; lateral-torsional buckling with the Cb factor, where a computed Cb of 2.7 was compared to the AISC equation value of 2.2 (AISC conservative) [54:33]; Saint-Venant (GJ) versus warping (Cw) torsion, showing a uniform-moment beam buckling at 2,265 in-kips with warping off versus 3,600 in-kips with warping on [62:17][66:19]; and second-order P-large-delta effects, where adding gravity load drove a column minor-axis moment from 722 to 1,042, a 44 percent increase [77:37][78:09]. A repeated teaching point is that an inflection point is not a brace point: a mid-span brace cut the unbraced length in half and raised capacity by 7.6x combined with the moment gradient [67:49][68:19]. MASTAN cannot model local buckling or unsymmetric angles; HSS and stocky box girders are fine if Z is input [16:17][47:24][93:09].

No erection, sequencing, crane, or rigging content. The only field-adjacent themes are conceptual: bracing reduces unbraced length and sharply raises buckling capacity [66:49], and out-of-plumb frame sway and member out-of-straightness are the two initial-imperfection types a stability model must capture [15:16]. These inform why erection plumb and brace placement matter but are not erection procedures.
### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
No inspection or rejection content (this is not a QA/QC lecture). The conceptual error the speaker most wants to correct is treating an inflection point as if it were a brace point, which overstates capacity [68:19]. A secondary modeling caution is using too few finite elements: one element badly under-predicts the buckling load, while eight elements land within a whisker of the exact solution [32:36][33:06].
