## Recent Developments in Design for Ponding
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lMeX1Ogym4k | Duration: 1:24:23 | Upload: NA | Tier: 2 | Watched: 2026-06-24
### Core Concept
Ponding is the progressive accumulation of rainwater on a flat, flexible roof where initial deflection traps water, the added water weight deflects the framing further, and the cycle either converges to equilibrium or runs away to collapse [03:42][04:13]. AISC defines it as retention of water solely due to roof deflection [03:08]. The talk distinguishes mitigation (provide enough slope so water drains and never accumulates), stiffness adequacy (keep actual member stiffness above a critical stiffness so deflections do not diverge), and strength adequacy (the framing can carry the ponded load) [07:46][08:16]. The governing physical quantity is critical stiffness, expressed as gamma times L to the fourth power over pi to the fourth, where gamma is the unit weight of water; when actual EI approaches that value the deflection amplifier runs to infinity [11:20][37:37][38:09].

### Spec References
Speakers cited a historical chain of AISC and load specs verbatim: the 1961 spec commentary (span-to-depth limit of FY over 1,000,000 and the 3-in-12 slope rule) [08:47][09:17]; the 1963 spec (FB over 600,000) [10:17]; the 1969 spec, which introduced the still-current Cp + 0.9 Cs less than 0.25 stiffness check and the 0.8 FY bending-stress limit, with a stated factor of safety of four against ponding instability [12:24][13:26][13:58]; the 1986 LRFD spec (Marino graphical aid moved into the main spec) [19:37]; the 1989 spec; the 1993 and 1999 specs (FY minus F0 change) [22:51]. Current provisions are AISC 360-05 and 360-10, Appendix 2, holding the simplified Cp + 0.9 Cs check and the Marino improved (graphical) method [28:14][29:17][59:45]. Load specs cited: ANSI A58.1 and its 1982 version, UBC 1994 (quarter-inch-per-foot safe harbor into main spec), ASCE 7-95, ASCE 7-10, IBC 2000 through IBC 2012 (susceptible bay), IBC 2015, and pending changes in AISC 360-16 and ASCE 7-16 [23:24][26:38][55:34]. Stability mandate references: Chapter C and Appendix 8 (second-order B1/B2 amplifiers), with member capacity taken from Chapter F [69:02][74:14]. SJI Technical Digest 3 was cited for camber and open-web steel joists, which Appendix 2 also addresses [02:38][63:23].

### Technical Details
The Appendix 2 math is a numerical solution of one specific geometry, not a general method: a simply supported beam with deflection described by a half-sine function (under 2 percent error versus the Euler-Bernoulli curve), framing into a girder that carries equal beams on both sides [35:33][41:13][42:16]. Cp and Cs are flexibility coefficients; the 0.25 limit derives algebraically from keeping the deflection amplifier from running to infinity with a factor of safety of four [48:22][49:23]. The pre-computer simplification stripped constants (pi to the fourth, 29,000 ksi E, water unit weight) into the lumped Cp/Cs constants, which is why those coefficients look non-intuitive [46:20][46:50]. The presenters could not derive the second strength equation in the spec and "banged our heads for about 10 days over it"; it ties deflection to stress through the elastic proportionality of stress, moment, and deflection, using 0.8 FY as the allowable stress whose source is also undocumented [50:24][51:26]. The cleaner modern path is to treat ponding as moment amplification (an alpha amplifier like B1/B2) using LRFD-factored loads, or an iterative first-order elastic analysis that accumulates beam-plus-girder deflections cycle to cycle [68:31][78:51][79:21].

### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
The recurring real-world error is treating the quarter-inch-per-foot slope as a guaranteed safe harbor; the speakers showed examples where a quarter inch per foot does NOT preclude ponding, especially where secondary members run parallel to the free-draining edge and there is a chain of stiffness toward an unyielding wall [27:09][29:17][32:58][67:30]. A second flagged error is engineers not accounting for the second-order added load: the rain load (5.2 times drain head per the cited formula) grows with roof-framing flexibility, and many designs ignore that increase [33:30]. The closing observation is that a pure ponding failure is rare; ponding usually acts as a contributing factor on top of another underlying deficiency rather than the sole cause [80:55][81:26].

### Bid / Cost Implications
No pricing, schedule, or estimating numbers were discussed. The practical cost lever is indirect: most economical real roofs fail the Cp + 0.9 Cs less than 0.25 check, so satisfying ponding stiffness can drive heavier members or added drainage, which affects tonnage [16:31]. The simplest fielded check is Hossler's rule of thumb cited at [64:23]: apply 5 psf and if deflection is under half an inch the roof is probably fine, over one inch it is probably unstable.
