## Partially Restrained and Flexible Moment Connections
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SdoZaCu7YLo | Duration: 1:09:25 | Upload: NA | Tier: 1 | Watched: 2026-06-24
### Core Concept
Dr. Louis Geschwindner (AISC VP of Engineering and Research, TR Higgins 2000) walks through the behavior, analysis, and a simplified design method for partially restrained (PR), semi-rigid, and flexible moment connections [00:30][01:32]. The unifying idea is the beam-line plus connection-curve intersection: a beam end develops a moment-rotation relationship from slope-deflection, the connection has its own moment-rotation curve, and equilibrium is where the two cross [19:37][20:09]. Because real connections behave nonlinearly up to a limit state (he shows a tested double-angle-plus-top-and-seat connection that ended in bolt failure), the design must verify the connection actually behaves the way the analysis assumed [18:35][41:24]. The closing "flexible moment connection" method is a modernized version of the old type-2-with-wind approach: design beams as simple, design connections for the lateral-load moment, then add explicit drift and stability checks [42:55][87:00].
### Spec References
AISC types of construction: type 1 rigid, type 2 simple, type 3 semi-rigid, introduced 1946 and carried through the 1989 ASD specification [03:08][03:40]. The 1986 through 1999 LRFD specification redefined these as type FR (fully restrained) and type PR (partially restrained) [05:11]. Section B3.6a defines simple connections and Section B3.6b defines moment connections (FR and PR) in the combined 2005 specification [06:44][07:16]. He cites the 13th edition AISC manual [06:13], the 2005 specification commentary on connection classification (and flags a likely typographical error there: it lists 20 and 2 where the referenced source paper used 0.5) [31:35][32:06]. The 90-percent and 20-percent fixed-end-moment classification thresholds are attributed to Omer Blodgett, "Design of Welded Structures," 1966 [29:01][29:32][31:35]. Source papers: Geschwindner, simplified PR beams, AISC Engineering Journal 1991 second quarter [13:25]; Geschwindner and Disque, "Flexible Moment Connections for Unbraced Frames Subject to Lateral Forces, a Return to Simplicity," EJ 2005 [43:26]; the three-parameter power model and connection databases by Goverdhan (1980s) and Kishi and Chen (1980s) [36:14][36:44]; Kim and Chen connection-stiffness measures in EJ [53:17]; Driscoll 1976 on stiffness ratios [55:22]; stability guidance from Joe Yura on leaning columns and a 10-story limit from a study by Mike Ackroyd [59:26][63:30].
### Technical Details
The beam-line equation comes from slope-deflection: Ma = 4EI/L times theta-a plus 2EI/L times theta-b, with the fixed-end moment wL-squared over 12 [15:29][15:59]. No matter what linear connection is used, a uniformly loaded beam can never be designed for less than wL-squared over 16, which matches the plastic collapse mechanism (wL-squared/16 at each end and at center) [23:48][24:50]. Classification uses the secant stiffness Ks: Ks times L over EI equals 18 for a fixed connection, 0.5 for a simple connection, and 2 at the 50-percent fixed-end-moment middle case [30:32][31:04][31:35]. Connections behave nonlinearly, unload along their initial stiffness, and after enough cycles reach "shakedown," after which they respond linearly within the maximum prior load [40:22][41:24]. Driscoll's three connection types: type A double web angle (0.2 to 0.76 of a rigid girder), type B top-and-seat angle (0.96 to 1.72), type C top-and-seat-plus-web (1.5 to 1.7); the flexible-moment-connection model treats one girder at twice its length as roughly 0.5, conservative for all but the most flexible connections [56:22][57:23][58:25].
### Fabrication Notes
This is an analysis-and-design webinar, so it carries no shop fabrication procedures, fit-up, or QC instructions. The relevant fabrication-adjacent point is the connection family itself: flush end plates, top-and-seat flange angles, double web angles, and flange plates are the typical PR connections, and a stated advantage is that moment capacity can sometimes be achieved with no welded component at all, which simplifies fabrication relative to fully restrained connections [09:49][10:20].
### Erection Notes
No erection sequencing, bracing, crane, rigging, or field-coordination content is presented. The speaker notes only the general claim that PR connections may be simpler to fabricate and erect than fully restrained connections, without procedure [10:20].
### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
The central failure mode shown is bolt failure in the connection at the top of its moment-rotation curve, the practical danger being that the three-parameter power-model equations imply the connection carries moment indefinitely when a real bolt or limit state ends it; designers must check that the connection does not run past its actual limit state [18:35][41:24][45:32]. He explicitly states he did not verify whether other limit states would govern the example connections, so they "may not actually work" [64:33]. He also flags a likely typographical error in the 2005 specification commentary classification numbers [32:06], and the historical misinterpretation that type-2-with-wind connections need not resist gravity moments, which the 2005 specification corrects by requiring the connection behave consistent with the analysis assumptions [50:11][51:13].
### Bid / Cost Implications
PR and flexible moment connections are presented as potentially more economical, with lower total steel weight and simpler fab and erection, but Geschwindner is candid that decades of AISC-sponsored fabricator cost studies comparing PR to FR frames reached no firm conclusion either way, so any cost advantage must be proven case by case [09:49][13:25][30:30]. The barriers to wider use are lack of authoritative literature, no clear guidance on whether they are economical, and no commercially available software [12:53]. For an estimator the takeaway is that a PR or flexible-moment-connection scheme is not automatically cheaper and requires its own engineering study before it can be priced as a savings.
