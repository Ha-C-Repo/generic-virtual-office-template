### Core Concept
Rafael Sabelli (Walter P Moore) opens the live portion of the AISC night-school series with the planning phase of seismic design for a four-story steel office building, special moment frames (SMF) in the long direction and buckling-restrained braced frames (BRBF) in the short direction [01:35][05:11]. The session covers system selection by seismic design category, effective seismic weight, period and base-shear determination, a wind-versus-seismic comparison to pick governing members once, the full set of horizontal and vertical irregularity checks, and construction of the seismic load combinations [04:09][62:17]. The unifying idea is capacity design: protect connections by forcing yielding into a controlled location (reduced beam section in the SMF, the brace core in the BRBF) and size everything else for the force that location can deliver [30:51][72:06].

### Technical Details
Materials: beams and columns A992 Fy 50 ksi with Ry; sections over 1.5 in flange thickness need specified CVN toughness, thinner flanges are exempt [19:03]. A913 Grade 65 (Grade 70 in 2016) permitted for SMF and BRBF columns [20:03]. Connection plates A36; base plates Grade 50, and plates over 2 in require CVN 20 ft-lb at 70F per AISC 341 [20:33]. BRB core is A36 with an added expected-yield window of 38 to 46 ksi so connections can be designed at about 42 ksi expected strength [23:37][24:37]. Weld filler is 70 ksi, CVN 20 ft-lb at 0F generally and 40 ft-lb at 70F for demand-critical welds where inelastic strain is expected, valid because lowest anticipated service temperature stays above 50F in conditioned space [22:36]. The RBS moves the plastic hinge off the column face and reduces beam stiffness, which is accounted for in the 2 percent drift check [30:51][32:21].
### Fabrication Notes
QC and QA come from AISC 360 Chapter N, with tables for welded and bolted joints listing tasks before, during, and after, plus NDT frequency rates [26:15][26:46]. AISC 341 Chapter J adds the more stringent seismic requirements over Chapter N, including demand-critical weld provisions, and AISC 358 adds requirements for the prequalified RBS [27:48][58:00]. Jurisdictions may approve a fabricator or erector to run QC in the shop without a parallel third-party QA inspector where the authority having jurisdiction allows it [26:46]. CVN testing is required only for plates over 2 in and sections with flanges over 1.5 in because thinner material reliably shows adequate toughness, so testing thin material wastes effort [19:33][80:47].
### Erection Notes
This planning session does not walk through field sequencing, crane, or rigging. The erection-relevant content is anchor-rod handling: Sabelli recommends specifying weldable Grade 55 anchor rods (supplement S1) so a too-short or mislocated rod can be field-fixed by welding an extension, with the standard fix shown and treated in AISC Design Guide 1 [21:04][21:34][22:05]. Grade 36 rods are always weldable and Grade 105 are never weldable [21:04]. Column splices sit above the second floor in this layout [06:43].
### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
BRB core material that comes in below 36 ksi is rejected for not meeting A36, and material between 36 and 38 or above 46 ksi is set aside because it falls outside the tightened expected-yield window [23:37][24:07]. A too-short or mislocated anchor rod is the named field problem, solved by the welded extension when weldable rods were specified [21:34]. The poll confirmed the common misconception that only one standard governs seismic QA: AISC 360 Ch N, 341 Ch J, 358 5.7, D1.1 Sec 6, and D1.8 Sec 7 all apply (answer F, all of the above) [76:41]. A second poll corrected the belief that aligning center of mass with center of rigidity removes torsional irregularity; accidental torsion still applies [78:14].
### Bid / Cost Implications
The governing-load logic is the cost message: size members for the controlling effect (seismic governs this building for both strength and overturning) and verify the other effect rather than designing twice, which cuts engineering effort [40:38][44:13][48:25]. BRBs are a tested proprietary system; 100 to 1000 kip braces are readily available, above 1000 kip they become special and availability plus test coverage must be verified, and manufacturers often assist with connection design [33:23][25:13]. Demand-critical welds, CVN toughness testing on thick plate, pretensioned bolts with prepared faying surfaces, and the added 341/358 QA all add fabrication scope and inspection cost that a seismic bid must carry.
