## Steel Castings in Architecture -- Do You Know How to Design Them?
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EswVvIhiSPM | Duration: 1:26:23 | Upload: NA | Tier: 2 | Watched: 2026-06-24
### Core Concept
This webinar teaches how to design and specify custom and standardized steel castings as monolithic connection nodes in architecturally exposed and fatigue-critical steel structures. The panel was Raymond Monroe of the Steel Founders Society of America, Carlos de Oliveira of a casting design firm, and design engineer Tom Meyer. The central idea is that pouring molten steel into a complex mold lets you put material exactly where the flow of forces wants it, replacing welded tube-to-tube junctions where stress concentration and weld defects normally cause fatigue failure [32:07][33:08]. A cast node moves the weld away from the geometric stress riser, which can extend fatigue life by an order of magnitude (a factor of 10) and last 10 times longer than a weld-fabricated connection [33:38][69:00]. Cast steel is real steel: all steel is cast, and a typical structural cast carbon steel runs about 30 ksi minimum to 40-plus ksi typical yield with 25 percent elongation, weldable under prequalified procedures with the engineer's approval [25:27][52:00].

### Technical Details
Cast nodes are non-prismatic disturbed-region (D-region) bodies where beam theory does not apply, so finite element analysis is required to find the stresses; the Charlotte airport job ran 1,044 FE cases across 32 cast nodes [50:07][51:08]. Geometry is tuned so wall sections taper to reduce stress at complex joints and so tubes framing into a node can be thinner-walled because the casting absorbs the local connection limit states (for example HSS chord-wall plastification), which reduces overall tonnage in tubular structures [34:40][35:42][54:09]. Castings are heat-treated and austenitized more than once to give equiaxed grains and uniform properties in every direction, unlike rolled product that retains directional sulfide and oxide stringers [83:26][84:28]. Common structural cast steel carries roughly 20 to 30 percent ductility; the firm can reach 180 ksi yield with reduced ductility, and grinding-media castings hit 300 ksi ultimate with essentially no yield [84:59][86:02]. Projects cited include Queen Richmond Centre Toronto (a 35,000 lb cellular cast node carrying about 20 million kip down each Delta frame, 70 ft tall, 40 in diameter, 2 in wall concrete-filled tubes) [41:20][44:26], University of Connecticut V-columns, Charlotte Douglas airport, and the Transbay Transit Center (34 castings, 5,000 to 50,000 lb each, an eccentric braced frame seismic system) [53:00].

### Fabrication Notes
A casting is built in a bonded-sand mold held with organic glue, with a gating (plumbing) system to admit metal, risers to feed shrinkage as steel contracts on solidifying, vents to release air, and a core to form internal passages [21:21][22:23]. Foundries use casting solidification and fluid-flow simulation to place risers and avoid shrinkage pockets, and additive sand printing to skip hard tooling and produce parts in one to two weeks [23:24][24:26]. After FE analysis sets the stresses, the casting designer sets non-destructive examination acceptance criteria zone by zone; any indication larger than the criterion is weld-repaired in process before final stress relief, yielding a sound monolithic part [50:38][51:08]. The team decides which features are as-cast (needing generous radii so detail is not lost) versus machined (counterbores and sharp-cornered reveals were machined at Charlotte for a clean aesthetic) [51:38][52:08]. Surface finish is matched to viewing distance and coating system, from MSS SP-55 for far views up to a machined or buffed mirror finish for close range; San Ysidro castings were galvanized then high-performance painted [54:09][82:55][77:12].

### Erection Notes
Cast nodes simplify field construction because they convert complex tube-to-tube T, Y, and K intersections into simple circumferential welds with good all-around weld access; a tree node would be shop-welded to the lower column and the upper branches field-welded as circumferential joints [37:46][38:16]. The Stuttgart Airport tree and similar structures show the casting taking the geometry so field welds are clean and accessible [37:15]. For the San Ysidro cable-stayed canopy, procurement and lead time were planned early so the custom castings (made overseas and shipped) would not become the critical-path item delaying erection [71:59][72:29]. Cable end fittings (open spelter sockets, conical sockets, head blocks) are themselves castings, connected by reverse-threaded rods for adjustable cable length rather than welding [64:35][65:39].

### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
The recurring failure mode named is fatigue cracking at welded tube junctions, where the weld sits at the peak geometric stress concentration and weld defects add their own stress riser, leading to failure after a number of load cycles [32:07][32:37]. Cast iron failures were described for contrast: graphitization, where acidic soil consumes the steel in gray iron pipe and leaves only continuous graphite that fails under load, plus gray iron's near-zero tensile ductility [13:38][14:09]. Inspection is by foundry NDE plus third-party NDE using radiographic, ultrasonic, magnetic-particle, and visual testing, with acceptance criteria and tensile testing of sample portions written into the spec; the warning is that the engineer of record should not set those criteria alone but with the casting designer [75:39][76:09].

### Bid / Cost Implications
Castings are not a cheap source of steel because the foundry spends as much effort on the shape as on the metal and works in small custom batches, but they are often the cheapest route to the final engineered component [20:48]. The strongest cost lesson is design-for-manufacturing: on Transbay the pre-design casting estimate was 22 million dollars on a 4 billion dollar project, and after a casting designer was engaged at 95 percent CD and redesigned plus added 46 castings across 15 new geometries, the final cost dropped 30 percent to 15.5 million [54:39][55:09]. Standardized off-the-shelf clevis and socket castings cut fabrication labor sharply: a fabricated aesthetic pin connection on a 6-5/8 in pipe needs about 102 linear inches of weld and grinding versus roughly a third of that for a circumferential weld of a clevis, weldable fast on a turning spindle with an automated machine [59:48][60:20]. Lead time spans bid-and-award, design, submittal-and-approval, manufacture, and shipping, so schedule must be planned early [72:00].

### Your Company Application
For Ivan's takeoff, the key insight is that cast nodes let tubular members be thinner-walled, so a structure with castings carries less tonnage than the equivalent all-welded tubular frame; do not price a cast-node job at the heavier weld-fabricated wall thickness, and treat custom castings as a delegated-design, performance-spec line with its own long lead time rather than a shop fabrication item. Mario's shop welding ties directly to the standardized-casting workflow: clevis and socket castings weld to HSS with simple circumferential welds (the spindle-plus-automated-welder method), far less deposit and grinding than a fabricated pin, and cast steel is weldable under AWS prequalified procedures only with EOR approval, so any cast-to-steel joint needs a WPS check. Paul's safety scope touches the heavy node picks (a 35,000 lb single casting at Queen Richmond, up to 50,000 lb at Transbay) for rigging and lift planning.
