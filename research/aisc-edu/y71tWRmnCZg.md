## Designing Members for Torsion
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y71tWRmnCZg | Duration: 1:35:57 | Upload: NA | Tier: 2 | Watched: 2026-06-24

### Core Concept
The webinar, presented by Dr. Daniel Linzell of the University of Nebraska Lincoln, teaches how to design steel members for torsion, with the focus on thin-walled open cross-sections (W-shapes, channels, angles, tees) that are torsionally weak [04:06][14:22]. The central message is that torsion in open shapes reduces to an elastic stress-analysis problem: you compute the angle of twist theta and its first, second, and third derivatives, convert those into St. Venant (pure) shear, warping shear, and warping normal stresses, then superimpose them with the direct axial, bending, and shear stresses and check against yield limits [34:58][37:33]. Key terms developed are shear flow, shear center (the point through which a load causes no twist), St. Venant torsion (planar, in-plane shear only) and warping torsion (out-of-plane flange movement that generates additional shear and normal stresses when restrained) [22:36][27:14][32:25].

### Spec References
AISC Steel Construction Manual Part 16 (the Specification), Chapter H combined-forces provisions, with HSS torsion handled in interaction Section H3.2 and open-shape torsion in Section H3.3 where the torsion term appears squared and torsion may be neglected if required torsion is under 20 percent of capacity for closed sections [35:29][36:31][37:02]. AISC Design Guide 9 (Torsional Analysis of Structural Steel Members), including its Section 2.3 statement that open shapes are torsionally poor, its Appendix A torsional properties (polar moment of inertia, warping constant, parameter a, warping statical moment, normalized warping function), and its Appendix B nomograph plots of theta and its derivatives by case [17:58][39:34][50:23][51:24][51:56]. The Q&A references Specification Section F2 (lateral-torsional buckling critical moment) [92:46]. Bridge-side references named: AASHTO LRFD Part 6 steel members, the NSBA/AASHTO Guidelines for Steel Bridge Analysis, and the AISC/NSBA Steel Bridge Design Handbook (warping figures in roughly Chapter 12) [20:33][56:01][57:34].

### Technical Details
The worked example is a W8x24, 50 ksi, 15 ft simply supported lintel over a bearing-wall opening, carrying CMU dead load plus an eccentric facade load applied 8 inches off the web centerline that produces a uniform applied torque tu [58:05][61:12][68:27]. Boundary conditions are taken as twist-pinned/warping-free (Appendix B Case 4): twist is prevented at the supports because the beam bears on block, but warping is left unrestrained as the conservative choice since end warping restraint could not be guaranteed [64:47][66:50][73:05]. Theta double prime at midspan is nonzero due to natural warping restraint from continuity, so warping normal stress exists where bending is maximum [74:39]. Computed results: strong-axis bending normal stress 8.5 ksi plus warping normal stress 9.5 ksi sum to 18 ksi against the 50 ksi yield limit; web direct shear 2.23 ksi plus pure-torsion web shear 4.67 ksi give about 6.9 ksi, while flange warping shear 0.45 ksi plus flange pure-torsion shear 7.62 ksi give about 8 ksi; the controlling 8 ksi is checked against 0.6Fy of 27 ksi, so the section passes [80:21][84:31][87:35]. The free Windows tool WinTorque (steeltools.org, version 1, DG9-era) computes theta and its derivatives and outputs two text files of twist values and element stresses [70:30][78:48].

The only fabrication-adjacent point is the recommendation to proportion a member so that torsional (pure-twist) buckling is not a concern rather than relying on a buckling check, since crucshaped sections are the weakest and are not normally used [38:34][39:04].

The closest field-relevant detail is that the lintel's torsional end fixity depends on whether the member bears tight against adjacent CMU with no gap, which controls whether warping is restrained; an open gap means warping is free [91:42][92:12].

### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
No inspection or rejection content. The recurring design pitfalls named are wrongly ignoring torsion when it cannot be explained away, mis-defining the warping boundary condition (twist-fixed versus twist-free changes the rotation curve and the warping stresses), losing track of stress signs when superimposing on non-I shapes since W-shapes conveniently always have a location where bending and warping stresses simply add, and a units slip in the example (kip-ft/ft label) that cancels out [18:28][64:47][41:38][76:11]. Several failure photos (a rigid frame collapse and the 2003 Marcy Bridge lateral-torsional/stability failure) are shown to argue torsion can be a real contributor, though not the sole cause [21:35].

### Bid / Cost Implications
No direct cost, schedule, or estimating content. The implicit takeaway for pricing is methodological: a member that looks adequate for vertical bending can be governed by warping, since in this torsionally weak W8x24 the warping normal stress (9.5 ksi) exceeded the bending stress (8.5 ksi) and roughly doubled the demand, so an eccentrically loaded lintel, spandrel, or facade-carrying beam may need a heavier section than a bending-only check suggests [80:52].

### Your Company Application
This maps directly to Ivan takeoff and the in-house PE stamp on any eccentrically loaded member: facade-carrying spandrels, CMU lintels, channel ledgers catching joist or bar-joist reactions off the shear center, and canopy framing where the load does not pass through the web all need a DG9 torsion check before the section is locked, because warping can double the stress demand and drive a heavier shape than aisc_validator.py weight selection alone would flag [19:31][42:11][80:52]. The free WinTorque tool plus DG9 Appendix A and B give the stamping engineer a defensible, low-cost path to compute theta derivatives and combined stresses, useful as a verification step on flagged eccentric members [70:30][51:24]. For Tekla detailing, the end condition matters: detailing a lintel tight against block with no gap can justify warping restraint and lower stresses, so the connection detail is a structural decision, not just a clearance choice [91:42].
