## Sustainability 2015 What's New with Steel and Sustainability
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tI88sRhdqBw | Duration: 1:15:59 | Upload: NA | Tier: 2 | Watched: 2026-06-24
### Core Concept
John Cross (AISC VP of Marketing) and Erica Winters Downey (Great Plains Regional Engineer) walk through the 2015 state of green building as it touches structural steel: the shift away from single-attribute material credits toward Environmental Product Declarations (EPDs), whole-building Life Cycle Assessments (LCAs), and the competing rating systems. The central argument for a fabricator is that steel is a multi-attribute material (recycled, recyclable, reusable), and that LCA quantities for steel must come from a licensed structural engineer's bill of materials, not a parametric estimator [46:38][47:10]. This is a market and documentation webinar, not a connection-design or AWS/RCSC technical session.
### Spec References
No AISC 360, AISC 341, AWS D1.1, RCSC, or AISC Design Guide sections are cited; the talk is about sustainability standards. The documents named are LEED 2009 and LEED v4 (LEED BD+C), ASHRAE 189.1, the International Green Construction Code (IGCC), Green Globes (an ANSI consensus standard in development), and the Living Building Challenge. ASHRAE 189.1 material path is stated as a comparative LCA or meeting two of four criteria: 10% recycled content, 15% regional materials, 5% bio-based, or 10 products with industry EPDs [50:18]. A Product Category Rule (PCR) under international standards governs the forthcoming steel EPDs [33:10].
### Technical Details
Recycled content for steel is calculated as post-consumer plus half of pre-consumer, by mass, with the credit contribution weighted by cost; 25% is the default claim with no documentation, though domestic mills publish actual figures online [22:20]. Regional materials use a 500-mile radius with three competing distance methods (mill-as-scrap-origin, scrap-allocation percentage, and actual transport distance where truck counts a full mile, rail one-third, barge one-half, ship one-fifteenth) [24:22][25:23]. AISC is producing three industry-average cradle-to-gate EPDs (hot-rolled sections, hollow structural sections, plate), built from a fabricator data survey of 1,100 members with 300 returns covering 1.1 million tons, showing about 7% fabrication waste and roughly 10 pounds of welding electrodes per ton of steel, with electricity as the dominant impact [33:42][37:54]. An industry-average EPD counts as only half a product toward credit thresholds.
### Fabrication Notes
The fabrication-shop data tied to EPDs is directly relevant: fabrication waste averaged about 7% of tonnage, consumables (welding electrodes near 10 lb/ton) and electricity drive the shop environmental impact, supplies generally come from within 50 miles of the shop, and steel scrap is sold, never landfilled, because buyers pay for it [37:23][37:54]. The cost basis for the recycled-content credit is material plus fabrication (material as delivered to site), not the mill price alone, and that is where Cross says fabricators most often submit LEED paperwork wrong [22:52].
### Erection Notes
Erection is named only to exclude it: the LEED material-cost basis is material plus fabrication and does not include erection [23:22]. No field sequencing, bracing, crane, or rigging content appears in this webinar.
### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
The recurring documentation error is fabricators reporting only the mill cost of steel rather than mill plus fabrication when claiming the recycled-content credit [22:52]. A second class of error is trusting parametric LCA estimators: the Athena estimator, run against 100 Steel Solutions Center projects, scattered with no pattern, underestimating steel by as much as 40% and overestimating by as much as 80% [44:05]. Cross argues an LCA that omits impact categories such as land use and habitat disruption is itself just a single-attribute assessment in disguise [47:40].
### Bid / Cost Implications
Building green adds cost (LEED Silver near parity historically, Gold or Platinum roughly 5 to 10% more), and the market now justifies green on operating-cost payback (about 11% first-year and 28% five-year operating reductions, seven-year payback targets) rather than principle [06:50][09:57]. For estimating, the load-bearing point is that structural steel quantity is a function of the framing system, bracing, and loads, not square footage, so a valid takeoff requires a licensed structural professional and cannot be backed out of a simple area sketch [45:07][46:38]. Material cost is defined as 45% of overall project cost in the LEED math, so a steel package near 9 to 10% of project cost already sits close to the 20% recycled-content threshold [23:22].
