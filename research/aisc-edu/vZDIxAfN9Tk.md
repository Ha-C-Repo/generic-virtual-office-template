## Moment Connections, Part 2

### Core Concept
Dr Brad Davis teaches fully restrained (FR) moment connections for wind and low-seismic (R=3) applications, focused on two bolted types that develop prying action: T-stub bolted moment connections and end-plate (moment-in-plate) connections [01:32][02:02]. The unifying idea is that negative moment is decomposed into a flange force couple, the tension flange force is delivered to the bolts through out-of-plane bending of the T-flange or end plate, and that bending can create fulcrum contact at the plate tips that pries the bolts so bolt force exceeds the static demand [02:32][03:33]. The governing design strategy is to size the plate for thick-plate behavior so the plate does not bend appreciably, no fulcrum forms, and the prying force Q goes to zero [05:38][11:55].

### Technical Details
Thick-plate behavior is confirmed when flange thickness reaches t-NP, derived by setting Mu = phi*Mp of the flange where Z = p*t^2/4 and Fu (not Fy) is used per the 14th edition calibration [12:26][13:30]. Key prying dimensions are b (stem face to bolt center), b' = b - db/2, a, a', and the tributary flange length p capped by the 1.75-to-1 load spread [08:44][10:21]. End-plate flexural strength uses yield-line analysis: the geometric yield-line parameter Y and yield-line location s are tabulated in Design Guide 16 (s is the same in every configuration) so the designer avoids the calculus minimization [44:30][59:26]. Three bolt behaviors exist: thick (Q=0, B=F), intermediate (B=F+Q), and thin (B=F+Qmax with four plastic hinges) [49:38][50:39]. The flush-plate 1.25 gamma factor (1/0.8) artificially raises usable plate strength so the connection classifies as FR; extended plates use gamma=1.0 [43:28][64:10].
### Fabrication Notes
The end plate is shop-welded transverse to the beam, then field-bolted to the column [35:14]. Weld design caps the beam-flange-to-end-plate weld at the larger of demand or 60 percent of flange strength to avoid an undersized weld in a high-stress region [60:29]. Web welds split into zone 1 (max of horizontal web stress or 60 percent of web strength) and zone 2 (sized for end shear) [61:32]. The worked example resolved to a quarter-inch double-sided fillet at the flange and 3/16 in double-sided fillets full-height on the web [73:32][75:36]. The recommendation is no weld access hole with CJP welds, plate width capped at flange width plus 1 in, and minimum bolt pitch of db plus 1/2 in (small bolts) or db plus 3/4 in (larger) to fit the tightening tool [64:42][65:15].
### Erection Notes
This webinar is design-focused and does not cover crane, rigging, sequencing, or field bracing procedures. The only erection-relevant point is that the end plate arrives shop-welded and is field-bolted to the column, and the design assumes compression-side bolts carry the full vertical shear (confirmed correct in the polling question, with friction at the joint cited as added margin) [77:38][78:44].
### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
Lab failures at Virginia Tech showed thin end plates and fin plates bending into fulcrums so bolts failed in combined tension and bending rather than straight tension, with plate deformation large enough to separate the plates by about six inches [04:05][04:35][51:42]. An MBMA test specimen mistakenly shipped with only two top bolts ruptured those bolts in straight tension with thick plates not bending [50:39]. The polling questions exposed two common misconceptions: that increasing bolt gauge or decreasing flange thickness reduces prying (both increase it), and the correct lever to reduce prying is higher specified Fu, which raises the no-prying threshold [79:47][81:18].
### Bid / Cost Implications
The thick-plate option yields a thicker plate with smaller-diameter bolts and a simpler design path; the thin-plate option gives the thinnest plate with bolts about one size larger and a much more complex calculation [55:47][58:22]. For estimating, the example showed light weld volume (a quarter-inch flange fillet and 3/16 in web fillets), so weld labor on a clean thick-plate moment connection is modest [73:32]. Choosing thick-plate trades a little more plate steel for fewer engineering hours and avoids the prying-force calculation entirely [56:18].
