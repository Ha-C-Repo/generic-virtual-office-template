## Designing for Membrane Architecture
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Br7_uNmmahI | Duration: 1:02:05 | Upload: NA | Tier: 2 | Watched: 2026-06-24
### Core Concept
This session, presented by Marko Kino, PE, covers the analysis, design, and fabrication of tensile membrane and pneumatic structures, not conventional building steel. The core idea is that a membrane structure must have three traits: Gaussian (double) curvature, elastic material that only reaches final shape after stretching, and tension-only loading where any compression, shear, or bending is treated as a failure [06:39][07:41][08:11]. The design workflow is a cyclical loop of form finding, loading, analysis, patterning, and detailing, where any downstream change in material or shape forces a return to an earlier step [08:42][09:46][32:29]. The two basic geometries taught are the cone (radial plus circumferential curvature) and the sail (a saddle that curves in two directions between high and low points) [04:38][06:39].

### Technical Details
Form finding uses software to find a minimal surface, the shape of least area between fixed boundary conditions, analogous to a soap film where stresses are equal in all directions [13:56][14:28]. The free tool "Form Finder" was named as an example [15:32]. Pre-stress magnitude controls final curvature: relaxing radial and circumferential stresses gives a subtle shape, increasing them gives a dramatic one [18:28][18:59]. Materials such as PTFE behave orthotropically (a woven cloth bonded between plastic layers), so an isotropic design assumption can produce wrinkles or failure, forcing a second analysis that decomposes stresses into orthogonal weave directions [30:57][31:29][31:59]. Manufacturers supply two values, a modulus of elasticity and an "apparent modulus of elasticity" that accounts for weave slack taken up before the material goes taut [34:35][35:06]. Theory of large deformations applies because deflections of six to twelve inches are normal, unlike steel where original and deformed geometry are assumed close [51:39][52:19].
### Fabrication Notes
Patterning converts a 3D curved surface into 2D strips, typically one meter (about three feet) wide because that is the standard roll width [27:50][30:27][55:56]. Cutting more material out of the gaps between strips produces more curvature but requires higher installation force [29:25][29:56]. Strips are joined by heat or infrared welding with a typical overlap of four to six inches sized to the force through the seam; epoxy and mechanical methods were tried but epoxies dissolved the membrane base material [36:08][38:11][38:41]. ETFE in particular must be welded in a dust-free sterile shop environment because it is clear, then folded and crated for shipment [48:55][49:26]. One Qatar stadium project requested a two-meter roll width to cut the number of seams and the welding labor [55:56][56:27].
### Erection Notes
The membrane is pre-stressed and tensioned into final shape in the field using cables threaded through fabricated edge pockets, interior or flying masts, or clamped steel edge plates pulled with jacks where pocket tearing is a risk [37:09][37:41][39:44][40:16]. Lifting is engineered like a tilt-wall panel: a lifting analysis confirms the pick points, and if reinforcement is needed a strong exterior patch is welded on and cut away after placement, never an interior reinforcement that would alter the designed cutting-pattern behavior [49:26][50:07][50:39]. Rain during installation is a major hazard because an untensioned membrane ponds water progressively, deforming further and accumulating more until it can fail before pumps clear it [20:00][20:30][21:01]. Pneumatic ETFE cushions hold shape by continuous internal air pressure with per-cushion pressure sensors and compressors that top up pressure as temperature and volume change [44:16][44:48].
### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
The dominant named failure is wrongly assuming the membrane braces the supporting steel. The Dallas Cowboys practice facility fabric roof was analyzed assuming the fabric braced the compression chords of light trusses on a light portal frame; with that assumed bracing everything passed, but removing it left members overstressed by 500 percent, and the collapse killed two people [25:13][26:17][26:48]. The fabricator had three or four similar failures and posted a website letter conceding its structures were designed incorrectly without the resources to fix them all [26:48][27:20]. Other errors named are isotropic assumptions on orthotropic woven material causing wrinkles or tearing [31:29], ponding during erection [20:30], and bird strikes or acid rain puncturing or degrading ETFE panels [57:00][57:30].
### Bid / Cost Implications
The speaker can estimate support-structure weight early for a minimal-surface shape, but a more dramatic architect-driven shape makes membrane stresses and therefore the steel support weight shoot up quickly [59:17][59:48]. Cost is also driven by material shelf life chosen up front, ranging from two-year temporary fabrics to materials rated 75 to 80 years that cost much more [52:19][58:37]. Specifying wider rolls trades higher material cost against far less welding labor and fewer seams [56:27]. Wind-tunnel testing is used only when a project is large enough to merit it, otherwise the engineer extrapolates conservatively from existing codes [19:30][20:00].
