## High Strength Bolting: The Basics
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PmXMTt_JPb8 | Duration: 1:34:51 | Upload: 2016-09-22 | Tier: 1 | Watched: 2026-06-24

### Core Concept
Geoffrey Kulak (author of AISC Design Guide 17 and the Guide to Design Criteria for Bolted and Riveted Joints) teaches the behavior and fundamentals of high-strength bolted joints, not connection design recipes. The structural engineer owns four jobs: selecting the bolt type and grade, designing the fastener, the installation, and the inspection [06:00]. Load transfers through a bolted joint in one of two ways, by shear in the bolt plus bearing on the connected material, or by friction (slip-critical), and the design philosophy throughout is to size against the ultimate (failure) load level because steel bolts have no clean elastic benchmark [34:00]. The lecture closes with a worked gusset-plate brace connection that walks slip load, bolt shear, plate bearing, block shear, and gusset buckling end to end.

Group A is A325/F1852 and Group B is A490/F2280 [slide 12].

### Technical Details
Minimum tensile strengths: A307 about 60 ksi, A325 120 ksi, A490 150 ksi with a 170 ksi ceiling because steel ductility falls off at that level [03:00-04:00]. Bolt shear strength is about 62 percent of bolt ultimate tensile strength, from the von Mises relationship 1/sqrt(3) = 0.577 confirmed by tests [33:00, slide 31]. The design nominal shear value takes 90 percent of that 62 percent, giving 0.563 Fu, which after threads-in-shear-plane and length effects lands at the tabulated Table J3.2 numbers (68 ksi for an A325 Group A bolt with no threads in the shear plane) [43:00]. Threads in the shear plane drop the value to about 80 percent of threads-excluded [44:00]. A bolt in tension uses the tensile stress area, about 0.75 of the gross pi*d^2/4 area, so P-ult = 0.75 Fu Ab [58:00]. Required pretension is 70 percent of bolt ultimate tensile strength [56:30]. Critically, ultimate bolt shear strength does not depend on pretension, because shear yielding in the bolt dissipates the installed pretension essentially to zero [40:00-42:00]. Slip resistance Rn = mu * Du * hf * Tb * ns per bolt, with mu = 0.30 Class A (clean mill scale, hot-dip galvanized, roughened) and 0.50 Class B (blast-cleaned), Du = 1.13 ratio of installed to specified-minimum tension, ns slip planes [slides 47, 109]. Plate bearing Rn = 1.5 Fu Lc t <= 3.0 d t Fu, reduced to 80 percent when deformation at the hole is a design consideration [slide 63, 61:00-62:00]. Block shear is tension fracture across the net tension area plus shear yield (about 0.6 Fy on the gross shear area) [65:00].

### Fabrication Notes
This is a fundamentals lecture, not a shop-process talk, so fit-up and QC are framed from the inspector's side. All material within the grip of the bolt must be steel; a non-steel layer (the lecturer cites the Kemper Arena failure, a resin-impregnated fiber pad within the grip) is barred and contributed to a collapse [16:00]. Faying surfaces are not painted in slip-critical zones to develop slip resistance [46:30]. Surface condition sets the slip coefficient, so the shop and coatings choice (clean mill scale versus blast-cleaned versus galvanized) directly fixes Class A versus Class B [slide 47]. Short, fat bolts (a 7/8 in A325 or A490 only 2 in long, especially galvanized) can twist off before installation completes because they lack enough thread length to absorb the turn; a rotation test is the check [76:00].

### Erection Notes
Installation starts snug-tight, the full effort of a worker on a 15 in spud wrench or the first impact of an impact wrench, bringing plies into firm contact working from the stiff center toward the edges [25:00, 69:00]. Pretensioning is by calibrated wrench, turn-of-nut, tension-control (twist-off) bolts, or load-indicator (direct-tension-indicator) washers [slide 24]. Turn-of-nut applies 1/3, 1/2, or 2/3 turn past snug depending on bolt length, half turn being typical, which intentionally yields the bolt so the least and most elongated bolts in a joint carry nearly identical tension (50 versus 49-51 kips example) [69:00-71:00]. Calibrated wrench must be calibrated on at least three bolts of that exact grip and re-calibrated when bolt length changes, and you must use washers [68:00]. A handbook torque-tension value is forbidden by RCSC because it is roughly 40 percent off [67:00]. Twist-off of the tip alone does not prove pretension, the calibration is what proves it [79:00]. Snug-tight contact need not be full across the whole joint, since slip resistance is independent of contact area (the inclined-plane physics) [48:00].

### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
Dividing total joint load equally by the number of bolts is wrong in long joints; an extreme 13-bolt-in-line case shows the end bolts take far more shear than interior bolts, which is why a length reduction applies past 38 in of joint [38:00-39:00, slide 40]. Ignoring prying action on bolts in tension undersizes them, prying can add 20 percent or more over P/n (the Kemper Arena lesson) [14:00-15:00, slide 16]. Fatigue cracks in slip-critical members start at edge nicks and scratches, not at the hole, so a nicked or poorly finished plate edge is a real reject in repetitive-load joints [50:00-51:00]. You cannot over-tighten a bolt; if it is over-tightened it simply breaks, so reports of broken bolts point to too-short bolts or insufficient ductility, not over-torque [76:00]. Inspecting for pretension that was never required wastes money, match the inspection to the joint class [72:00].

### Bid / Cost Implications
Do not default to the most conservative joint. Specifying slip-critical or pretensioned bolts where snug-tight bearing would serve costs more to install and more to inspect; field-measured slip is about half a hole clearance (one dime thickness), tolerable for most buildings [29:00-30:00]. Bolt grade and faying-surface choice carry installed cost: tension-control twist-off bolts install from one side, quietly, and need no separate inspector watching every bolt, but the bolts themselves cost more [79:00-80:00]. Turn-of-nut delivers measured pretension averaging 80 percent of ultimate for A325 (about 38 percent over the specified minimum), so the conservatism is already built into the rule and need not be paid for twice [78:00]. The estimating takeaway is to specify the lowest joint class the loading allows.
