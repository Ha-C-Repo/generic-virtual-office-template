## Introduction to Seismic Connections
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gqCdO8zB0gA | Duration: 1:33:24 | Upload: 2020-03-04 | Tier: 1 | Watched: 2026-06-24
### Core Concept
This is AISC Live Webinar Session 3 of the "Connection Design for Moment Frames and Braced Frames" series, taught by Dr. Owner Atherton of Virginia Tech, on the design of steel seismic connections. The governing theme is ductility, defined at [02:14] as the ability to deform inelastically without significant loss of strength, with structural steel reaching a material ductility ratio of 100 to 200 [02:46]. Seismic design works because an earthquake acts more like an applied displacement than an applied force [03:29], so as system ductility rises the required strength and material quantity fall while connection detailing, welding, and inspection cost rise [04:19]. The talk drills into two systems, the special moment frame (SMF) and the special concentrically braced frame (SCBF), and the connection rules that flow from capacity design and the post-Northridge testing regime.

### Technical Details
SMF conformance requires full-scale cyclic testing to 0.04 radians (4 percent story drift) while retaining 80 percent of nominal plastic moment, provable three ways: an AISC 358 prequalified connection, a connection prequalified by others, or project-specific qualification testing [12:26, 73:01]. The 358 menu includes the reduced beam section (RBS), bolted and welded end-plate, bolted flange plate, and welded unreinforced flange-welded web (WUF-W), plus proprietary types. The worked RBS example (W36x150 beams, W14x455 column) computes the probable maximum moment Mpr using Cpr (1.15 for A992), Ry (1.1), and the effective plastic modulus, projects it to the joint center, and verifies strong-column-weak-beam at a ratio of 1.04 [frame 10, transcript]. The SCBF example designs a brace connection for an HSS 6.875x0.500 A500 Gr. B brace (Ag 9.36 in2, Fy 42 ksi, Ry 1.4), giving Tu = 550 kips and a capacity-design compression demand of 444 kips, sized through a 5/8 in A572 Gr. 50 gusset checked for Whitmore yielding, block shear (970 kips), and gusset buckling (599 kips), with a 2t fold line of 1.25 in and a 1 in by 1 in reinforcing bar to restore effective net area [frame 16, 60:00 to 72:18].
### Fabrication Notes
The RBS flange cut is a shop operation that relocates the plastic hinge away from the column weld [transcript ~RBS]. End-plate connections are shop welded with no weld access holes, which the speaker notes gives well-controlled welds [transcript]. The beam web to column flange is a CJP weld detailed per 358 section 5.6 using a 3/8 in shear tab as weld backing [frame 11]. The brace-to-gusset slot is cut 1/8 in wider than the gusset thickness, which reduces the brace net section and usually forces added reinforcement [67:44]. The knife-plate variant lets the brace slot weld around the plate end to make the effective net area equal the gross area, avoiding reinforcement, at the cost of one more piece and more welds [56:48, 91:24]. Continuity plates are required when column flange thickness is below the t-limit (in the example tcf 0.855 in versus tc-lim 1.50 in, so plates required) [frame 11].
### Erection Notes
The video is design and detailing focused and does not walk through field sequencing, crane, or rigging procedures. The one field-relevant geometry point is the 2t (or elliptical) fold line in the gusset, a clearance zone that must stay clean so the gusset can hinge and accommodate brace rotation; the speaker advises against letting bolted beam-gusset angles encroach on that fold line because restraining the hinge is a bad idea [82:00, 56:48]. Bolted brace connections may need slip checked only for oversized holes, where slip demand can be capped by overstrength loads; standard holes do not require slip-critical design here [frame 14].
### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
The Northridge 1994 earthquake produced fractures in hundreds of SMF buildings, typically at the beam bottom-flange to column-flange weld [11:25]. Named contributing causes were the bottom-flange backing bar acting as an effective crack starter, poor weld quality, the composite slab effect, low-toughness weld filler metal, and weak panel zones that kinked the column flanges [12:02]. The post-Northridge fix is mandatory full-scale qualification testing for all SMF and IMF connections [12:26]. Protected zones (middle quarter of the brace, brace ends, and the entire gusset connection per 341 F2.5c) allow no attachments, welds, or defects, to prevent premature fracture in yielding regions [59:05]. Demand-critical welds at column base plates and beam-to-column joints carry additional filler-metal toughness requirements [58:23].
### Bid / Cost Implications
The speaker is explicit that higher-ductility systems trade material for connection cost: in high-seismic zones the material savings outweigh added connection and inspection cost, but in low-seismic zones (East Coast, Midwest, where wind often governs) the connection detailing cost can exceed the material saved, making an R=3 system without seismic detailing more economical [04:24, 78:00]. System choice drives R factor (6, 7, 8 for high ductility) and therefore both tonnage and detailing scope. For estimating, seismic connections add full-scale-tested prequalified detailing, demand-critical weld filler metal, continuity plates, gusset reinforcement bars, and added NDE, all cost that does not appear in a plain gravity-frame takeoff.
### Your Company Application
For Ivan takeoff, a seismic project means tonnage is only part of the story; the bid must carry continuity plates, gusset reinforcement bars, knife plates, and the labor of demand-critical CJP welds, so flag any drawing set noting AISC 341, 358, SMF, SCBF, IMF, or "demand critical" and price the connection labor separately rather than folding it into a flat per-ton rate. For Mario and AWS D1.1 welding, demand-critical welds require higher-toughness filler metal and the protected-zone rule bars any tack, attachment, or arc strike in the brace mid-quarter, brace ends, and gusset, which the shop and erectors must respect. For Tekla detailing, the 2t fold-line clearance, the 1/8 in brace slot oversize, the 3/8 in shear tab used as weld backing, and the SH plastic-hinge offset are modelable detail dimensions taken straight from AISC 358. For the in-house PE stamp, only a stamped engineer selects the system, runs strong-column-weak-beam, panel-zone, and capacity-design checks; the takeoff tool verifies member weights through aisc_validator.py and never generates these seismic capacities. Houston is low-seismic, so most Your Company work, including ACP, will not be SMF/SCBF, but the cost lesson is the standing rule: confirm the seismic system before pricing connections.
