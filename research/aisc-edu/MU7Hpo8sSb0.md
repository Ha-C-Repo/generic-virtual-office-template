## What Your Fabricator Wishes You Knew About HSS
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MU7Hpo8sSb0 | Duration: 56:13 | Upload: NA | Tier: 1 | Watched: 2026-06-24

### Core Concept
Kim Olson of Force Consulting, technical consultant to the Steel Tube Institute, presents fabricator feedback gathered from five AISC-certified shops and SimCorp on why HSS gets a reputation for being expensive. The thesis is that HSS material is often economical (HSS columns run roughly pound-for-pound equal to wide flange, and the lack of a weak axis gives more efficient column use), but the savings get thrown out the window through poorly considered connection details and standard details copied from set to set [03:00, 04:54]. The recurring lesson is design the member with its connection and tube wall tolerances in mind, because the local wall strength of a flexible HSS at the joint can control the connection capacity, unlike a wide flange support [48:00, frame_0021].

### Spec References
The speaker cites these by name. ASTM A500 (current structural tube spec, permissible-variation tolerance tables) and its 0.93 design wall factor tied to AISC B4.1 [49:41]. ASTM A1085 (newer spec replacing A500, tighter wall tolerance, single yield Fy 50 to 70 ksi, removes the 0.93 factor, CVN 25 ft-lbs at 40 F) [49:22, frame_0022]. ASTM A847 appears on the tolerance-table slide alongside A500/A1085 [frame_0019]. ASTM A53 (coated round pipe, advised against in favor of A500) [39:39]. AISC 360 Chapter K (HSS connection checks behind the STI HSS Connex tool) and AISC 360 reduced bearing values for pin-type bolted connections [48:37, 32:00]. AWS D1.1 limited effective throat for flare-bevel groove welds, and the 6G welder certification for tube-to-tube CJP without backing [18:33, 28:12]. AISC Code of Standard Practice Section 10 governs Architecturally Exposed Structural Steel, with Section 10.2.8 stating HSS seam welds are acceptable as produced [37:00, 38:43]. Slide footer cites the HSS Design Guide: Section Properties and Design Information, and Packer and Henderson Figure 3.28 for overlap detailing [frame_0019, frame_0011]. Bolts shown on details are A325-N [frame_0004, frame_0006].

### Technical Details
Four wide-flange-beam-to-HSS-column shear connections were ranked least to most expensive: shear tab (cheapest), WT, double angle, then through plate (about four times the cost of a single plate) [06:46, 08:48]. Four moment connections of similar capacity were compared: PJP weld to the face (most economical, needs 5/8 inch wall), CJP weld, through plate, and cutout/wrap plate; the through and cutout options allow a thinner wall near 3/8 inch [12:55, frame_0006]. For trusses the matched connection that architects prefer is harder and costlier than a stepped connection where branch members land on the tube flat, defined as the width minus four times the wall thickness [17:01]. Round HSS in trusses can roughly triple connection cost versus rectangular [24:10]. Larger and thicker tubes carry bigger corner bend radii, and radius varies producer to producer, which matters when welding two tubes end to end [29:42].

### Fabrication Notes
A500 outside-flat tolerance is 0.020 in up to 2.5 in, scaling to 0.01 times the large flat dimension above 5.5 in; the slide gives an H/B ratio table for square and rectangular tube [42:54, frame_0019]. Worked example: an HSS 12x4 can vary about 0.18 in across the 4 in face and 0.12 in across the 12 in face, so two tubes can stack a quarter inch of slop at an end-plate joint [43:25]. Round tube out-of-round tolerance is 0.75 percent of OD over 2 in; straightness is 1/8 in per 5 ft [45:12, 45:51]. A tube-to-tube fillet weld is about two hours of fit-up; the same joint as a CJP is one to one-and-a-half days plus inspection [29:01]. Push fillet welds up to about 1/2 in before stepping to PJP, then CJP only if required [27:46]. Laser/profile cutters and robotic welders (common in Japan for round HSS columns and moment frames) are lowering tube fabrication cost [25:53, 26:41]. Specify removal of the inside flash weld and the seam-weld orientation explicitly, since neither is a default [38:43].

### Erection Notes
Erection content is light. HSS gives tighter dimensional control and resists twisting during handling and erection, which is why it suits AESS [38:03]. Double-angle shear connections may need the beam double-coped for erectability [07:58]. Temporary bolted erection aids are normal for field-welded AESS and can be removed; the Japanese robotic system welds stub beam/stub column assemblies in the shop, flies them out, and field-connects an infill beam between columns [26:41, 41:03]. A questioner noted HSS connections lend themselves to shop welding then field bolting because of limited access to the tube interior; the presenter agreed much shown work can move to field welds [52:01].

### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
Through-plate connections passed from set to set as a default detail are the headline error, including a case where an all-around weld symbol forced welding the through plate inside the tube, so the shop chopped off the column top and rewelded it, and a crucifix two-direction through plate that required cutting the column in half [09:46, 10:42, 11:00]. Sizing welds to develop full member yield strength rather than the actual applied load is a common overdesign that drives cost [22:00]. Specifying tube-to-tube CJP welds where fabricators report the inspection often is not done or is done improperly [28:39]. Putting all welds ground smooth on AESS drawings is called out as a needless cost; the Code of Standard Practice only asks for a reasonably smooth and uniform weld [41:11]. Overlapped truss joints detailed per the unacceptable case in Packer Figure 3.28 (missing the hidden weld plate at the through member) lose strength [frame_0011].

### Bid / Cost Implications
A single through plate adds a full extra hour of shop time per connection; on a frame with forty or fifty of them that compounds fast, and the speaker notes bumping a 3/16 in wall to 1/4 in to avoid a through plate is often cheaper overall [10:00, 09:27]. CJP and through-plate moment joints are the two priciest options once material, fit-up, and inspection are included; the tube-to-tube CJP swing from two hours to over a day per joint is the clearest schedule hit [16:01, 29:01]. Round HSS roughly triples truss connection cost, though cost per pound for round versus rectangle is the same for equal A500 sections, so an efficient round can still win on total price [24:10, 53:51]. AESS under Code Section 10 carries a real premium and should be specified only where the element is genuinely close to view [37:00].
