## Solutions for Vibration Issues-Evaluation and Retrofits
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x5aIywz1XI0 | Duration: 1:26:35 | Upload: NA | Tier: 2 | Watched: 2026-06-24

### Core Concept
This webinar by Dr. Tom Murray (first author of AISC Design Guide 11) and Dr. Brad Davis teaches how to evaluate and retrofit floors and other structures that vibrate excessively under human activity. The controlling distinction is the "low frequency floor" with a natural frequency below about nine Hertz, where an occupant walking at a subharmonic of that frequency drives the floor into resonance and produces the maximum felt response [08:19][08:51]. The retrofit decision rests on the equivalent sinusoidal peak acceleration (ESPA), not the raw peak particle acceleration, compared against the tolerance limits in Design Guide 11 [14:59][16:33]. The repeated message is that retrofit design must begin with field measurements rather than calculation alone, because predictions are only moderately accurate [74:40][75:10].

### Spec References
The speakers cite AISC Design Guide 11, Second Edition (floor vibration, published roughly two years before the talk) and its chapters: Chapter 2 (tolerance limits) [31:36], Chapters 4 and 5 (manual prediction methods, the "gold standard" used over 20 years) [40:17][57:16], Chapter 6 (finite element and sensitive-equipment criteria, including the 1/3 octave velocity limits) [35:42][38:15], and Chapter 7 (the FRF method, with a Version 2 in development) [49:02]. Equation 6-38 of Design Guide 11 is cited by number for the combined resonant-plus-impulse walking response [40:17]. SJI Technical Digest No. 5 is named as an alternate manual method [04:41]. Dunkerley's equation is cited for combining joist and girder frequencies [24:52]. No AISC 360, 341, AWS D1.1, or RCSC sections were stated in this floor-serviceability talk.

### Technical Details
Natural frequency is found in the field by a heel drop (rise on the balls of the feet, strike with the heels), which has frequency content to about 40 Hertz, then processed by FFT to a response spectrum [06:46][07:16]. Walking tests use a metronome set to a step frequency in the 1.6 to 2.2 Hertz range, chosen so an integer multiple equals the floor natural frequency (for a 6 Hz floor, divide by 3 to get a 2 Hz gait) [09:52][10:23]. Records are bandpass filtered between roughly 1 and 15 to 20 Hertz because humans are insensitive outside that band, and 30 or 60 Hz equipment noise is filtered out [13:55]. ESPA is computed by taking the maximum rolling one-second RMS acceleration and multiplying by the square root of 2 [15:30][16:02]. The quiet-environment tolerance is 0.5 percent of gravity; the lab/microscope limit was 4,000 micro-inches per second on a 1/3 octave velocity basis [17:38][35:42]. Humans perceive vertical displacements as small as ten thousandths of an inch, so any retrofit element must be very stiff [19:10].

### Fabrication Notes
The fabricated retrofit members are conventional rolled shapes welded to existing framing. Examples named verbatim: W10x15 welded 20 feet long to joist bottom chords [27:29]; W16x26 placed between joists near midbay with HSS pieces welded between the girder top flange and the deck underside to force composite girder action [40:48][41:19]; and cold-formed channels (the heaviest 12-inch channels available) plus shorter top-chord channels added to light steel trusses for added mass and stiffness [42:50][51:08]. Reinforcement is welded only at the joist panel points [28:32]. Because the elements add both mass and stiffness, mass-only blocks (the owner's 1-foot concrete cubes) changed the response by only about one hundredth of a percent and were a waste [24:21][25:53].

### Erection Notes
Field installation is the hard part. To strain the new member into the load path, the crew jacks up the floor about a half inch, then welds the reinforcement to the bottom chord or bottom flange before releasing the jacks [19:41][29:03]. Joists were stiffened a set of three or four at a time across the whole bay, which required removing the ceiling first [28:32][29:03]. Welding in an occupied building is rarely feasible and is risky around computers, so tenants below were relocated to temporary space during the work [20:12][28:32]. Other field fixes shown were hidden posts disguised as electrical or communication drops (which must run to grade or they only move the problem to the floor below) [21:16][21:46], HSS 3x3 columns at cubicle corners to raise frequency [58:52], and tuned mass dampers installed between a raised access floor and the slab [70:51].

### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
The named diagnostic errors are using peak particle velocity or peak particle acceleration instead of ESPA, which overestimates what a human feels (an 0.875 percent G peak against a 0.531 percent G ESPA) [14:59][30:36]; relying on long-term monitoring without paired video so the cause of each event is unknown [31:36]; and using cell-phone accelerometers, which lack control of sampling rate, voltage range, and bandwidth and are not sensitive enough at the very low floor levels [32:37]. Real design misses included an engineer who eyeballed a hotel crossover (W16x31, 30 ft) as fine, predicting 9.35 Hz and 1 percent G but getting 4.71 Hz and 4.4 percent G [64:34][65:39]; a monumental stair on wire-rope-supported landings where the EOR added a few secondary members without a vibration check and got 3.25 percent G under four people descending [67:11][68:14]; and resonant cross-floor transmission where dancing six floors below shook tenth-floor offices through axial column deformation [61:30][63:02].

### Bid / Cost Implications
Retrofits in occupied buildings carry heavy soft costs: tenant relocation, ceiling removal and reinstatement, jacking, and after-hours welding around live computers, which the speaker repeatedly calls "not cheap" and sometimes infeasible [20:12][28:32]. The cost message for estimating is that mass-only fixes (concrete blocks or cubes) are wasted money because they do not move the response, while the effective fix is stiff welded reinforcement that adds mass, moment of inertia, and transverse stiffness together [20:44][25:53]. Field measurement campaigns (multiple walkers, multiple paths, seismic accelerometers, FE calibration) are an engineering-service line item that should precede any retrofit pricing.
