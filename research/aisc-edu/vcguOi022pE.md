## Entertaining With Steel, From Theatrical Rigging to Concert Touring Stages

### Core Concept
Bill Goren of McLaren Engineering Group's entertainment division walks through why structural steel is the controlling material for the heaviest entertainment work: theatrical rigging, concert touring stages, themed sculptures, amusement rides, and animatronic show structures [02:03][03:34]. The recurring theme is that aesthetics, motion ranges, and tight field tolerances drive the geometry, and steel earns its place over aluminum and wood on stiffness, fatigue resistance, ductile failure, impact resistance, weldability with low distortion, and worldwide availability for touring [09:13][09:44][10:16]. Steel is chosen even where it is heavier because it survives being thrown around on trucks and welds with less warping than aluminum [10:49][47:16].

### Technical Details
Connection and system types span pinned connections favored for speed of erection over A325/A490 bolts [08:11], custom tubular cross sections fabricated from plate cutouts with rolled sides joined tab-in-slot like a jigsaw [26:21], internally sleeved column splices with flat countersunk bolts in tapped holes to hide fasteners under cladding [26:52][28:57], ring (crane) bearings five feet in diameter standardized across four turntables so they could be ordered together [35:41][36:13], and slip-critical A325 bolted moment connections forming a portal frame in a theater [53:33]. Member sizing leans on tubes for the strongest cross section under strange multi-axis and torsional forces, with finite-element modeling of body segments on the dragon and cable-stay vibration analysis tuned with pretension so foe rods would not flutter except in extreme wind [16:30][19:35].
### Fabrication Notes
Tolerances on themed work exceeded architecturally exposed structural steel requirements: the treehouse at Crystals had tighter tolerances than AESS and allowed no exposed bolts, forcing nuts inside beams with access holes and detailer-modeled wrench reach to make up the connections [26:21][28:23][28:57]. The Macau dragon bull-wheel center had to land inside a one-inch-radius sphere 60 feet in the air, met with spherical washers, Teflon alignment pads, longitudinal slots, and Enerpac hydraulic pancake actuators adjusting under load before tie rods locked it off [15:59][17:01][18:04]. Wall sections were replaced with thick local plates welded from both sides to handle concentrated forces, and lift machines were detailed for removal through a hatch with four bolts for service [29:27][36:45].
### Erection Notes
Erection sequencing is brutal on these structures because geometry, balance, and access fight back. The dragon used temporary shores under the neck, then a controlled handoff where releasing a pin and removing a strut confirmed load transfer to the cable stays and bull-wheel rope [18:04][18:34]. Touring practice favors pins over bolts because a pin takes thirty seconds versus three minutes for a bolt, and across many connections that delta makes or breaks a show schedule; U2's tour leapfrogged three claw structures across stadium sites so one was always being finished while shows ran [08:11][08:41]. In a theater no crane can enter, so a mast-mounted arm with a chain motor lifted an aluminum truss assembly and lift crews bolted it to column tops [53:03][53:33].
### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
No formal rejection categories or inspection-failure types are enumerated. The named risk is loss of quality control on overseas work: the Sentosa crane dance had the engineer of record value-engineered out, leaving the team unsure whether paint and welding QC requirements were followed [44:07]. Aluminum was rejected for the salt-water animatronics because welded aluminum carries fatigue problems, lacks an endurance limit, and demands higher welding QC than fewer qualified Asian fabricators could reliably hit [47:16][47:46]. The fee-estimating caution was to price carefully and conservatively and re-price after schematic design [22:12].
### Bid / Cost Implications
Fees on one-off entertainment structures are estimated conservatively at schematic design and revised once the final design is known, sometimes with a staffed presence through fabrication and installation written into scope [22:12][22:43]. Connection method directly drives the schedule budget: pins over bolts to compress load-in and load-out, since on the Roger Waters tour a million-dollar flying rig added 45 minutes to load-out and got cut because the act could not afford the time [55:35][56:06]. A 30-day temporary-structure limit being exceeded reclassified a Seattle show as a permanent building, triggering higher importance factors and a code fight before fire-treated lumber was allowed [52:02][52:33].
