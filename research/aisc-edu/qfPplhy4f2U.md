## More Opportunities - Design by Inelastic Analysis
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qfPplhy4f2U | Duration: 1:31:45 | Upload: NA | Tier: 2 | Watched: 2026-06-24

### Core Concept
This is the eighth and final lecture of an AISC Night School course on stability design, presented by Dr. Ronald Ziemian of Bucknell with Dr. Donald White, covering design by inelastic analysis under AISC 360 Appendix 1 [02:38]. The method extends the direct analysis method one step further by directly modeling inelastic material yielding rather than running a second-order elastic analysis and then checking member equations [03:08]. The controlling idea is that if the analysis program itself captures a given limit state behavior (flexural buckling, lateral-torsional buckling, partial yielding from residual stress), the engineer does not have to satisfy the corresponding equation in the main body of the specification, because the computer acts as a virtual lab predicting the structure's full limit-state response [38:39][42:48]. Ziemian frames this as economy, simplicity, and rationality, the same three arguments Lynn Beedle made for plastic design in 1956 [16:32].

### Spec References
The talk centers on AISC 360 Appendix 1, "Design by Inelastic Analysis," added in the 2010 specification, described as a five-page appendix with eight pages of commentary structured in three parts: general requirements, ductility requirements, and analysis requirements [41:16]. Speaker cites the main body chapters A through N as the equation set you fall back to, specifically the Chapter E column curve, the Chapter F beam curve, and the Chapter H beam-column interaction equation [37:36][43:18]. Chapter C is referenced as the home of the direct analysis method (DM) for elastic analysis [34:31]. The speaker notes Appendix 1 does not apply to ASD and does not cover seismic, directing seismic inelastic work to AISC 341 [42:48]. The Code of Standard Practice is named as a base for geometric imperfection tolerances [88:51]. Historical references include the 1961 AISC plastic design appendix (Part 2) and a 1967 Engineering Journal paper on a 14-story Binghamton apartment building [17:32][21:05].

### Technical Details
The plastic hinge is the core mechanism: a section yields fully at its plastic moment Mp and must keep resisting that moment through a rotation theta-p while load redistributes [49:57]. Appendix 1 requires a rotation capacity of about 3, meaning Mp is maintained for roughly three times the rotation needed to first form the hinge [50:28]. The appendix is limited to doubly symmetric shapes; singly symmetric or non-symmetric shapes require a program sophisticated enough to capture twist and torsion [65:51]. For members with high axial force and low bending, Appendix 1 caps usage at 75 percent of CPy because ductility for multiple hinges is uncertain past that point [66:21]. Analysis inputs that must be modeled include second-order effects, initial geometric imperfections (out-of-plumb, out-of-straightness, often via notional loads or scaled buckling-mode shapes), partial yielding, and residual stresses, with E and Fy reduced by 0.9 as the phi-factor reduction [51:30][89:30].

### Fabrication Notes
This is an analysis and design webinar, not a shop-process talk, so there are no direct fabrication, fit-up, or shop QC procedures discussed. The closest fabrication-relevant content is the demand for compact sections and adequate ductility, which is a member-selection and detailing constraint rather than a shop instruction [77:43]. Residual stresses from rolling and fabrication are named as a modeled effect, with the profile depending on cross-section geometry and adopting published models such as Galambos-Ketter or the European model [87:48].

### Erection Notes
No conventional erection, sequencing, crane, rigging, or field-bracing procedures are taught here. One real-world example touches construction-stage analysis: SDL in Atlanta used advanced (ANSYS) analysis to study twin girder outrigger beams forming a temporary structure that supported large trusses cantilevering over a historic stone building during the construction sequence [67:22]. That illustrates inelastic analysis applied to a temporary erection support condition rather than a standard erection method.

### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
No bolt, weld, or NDE inspection failures are discussed; this is not an inspection webinar. The relevant failure modes are analytical limit states: local buckling at a plastic hinge, lateral-torsional buckling, and loss of stiffness leading to a frame instability mechanism [77:11]. The central caution is that a "frame analysis" line-element program that does not capture partial yielding and residual stress (called "frame analysis 1" in the demo) will overstate strength, so the engineer must fall back and check the Chapter E, F, or H equation [45:21]. The late Theodore Galambos urged a "skull and crossbones" on page one of Appendix 1 because an underqualified engineer who does not understand nonlinear behavior can misuse it [73:35].

### Bid / Cost Implications
The economic case is that inelastic analysis recovers steel weight that elastic methods leave on the table by allowing controlled hinge formation and force redistribution. In one portal-frame example the structure carried about 35 percent more load before reaching its limit state versus the direct analysis method governing at 65 percent [62:47]. The speaker is candid that the savings are not always large: in a more redundant frame the gain was only about 8 percent, since the direct analysis method already recovers most of the available capacity as a "poor man's inelastic analysis" [64:49]. Software cost is a real factor: nonlinear-capable frame programs cost more, and full shell-element programs (Abaqus, ADINA, ANSYS) get very expensive [80:51].

### Your Company Application

It is a niche tool for unusual structures where effective length and unbraced length are undefinable (a freestanding arch bandstand, ladder columns that are a webless stocky W12 with two-inch flanges, twin-girder outriggers over a historic building) [66:51][70:58].
