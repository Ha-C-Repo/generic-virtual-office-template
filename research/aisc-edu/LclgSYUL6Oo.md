## Design of Reinforcement for Steel Members - Part 1
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LclgSYUL6Oo | Duration: 1:31:38 | Upload: NA | Tier: 1 | Watched: 2026-06-24
### Core Concept
This webinar by Bo Dowswell, P.E., Ph.D. of ARK International covers the strength and stability design of structurally reinforced existing steel members, specifically columns under flexural buckling and beams under lateral torsional buckling [01:03]. Reinforcement enlarges the cross section to carry added load (building extension) or to restore strength lost to damage, corrosion, or fatigue [02:03], [02:33]. The two governing themes are the controlling effect of geometric imperfections (rolling tolerance, P-delta, and weld distortion) on a reinforced member's strength [10:40], and whether reinforcement is "stabilizing" (radius of gyration of the reinforced section at least 85% of the original), which lets the designer neglect preload [32:36], [67:08].

### Spec References
The speaker cited AISC 360 (2010) Section E3 flexural buckling with the inelastic and elastic critical-stress equations, the elastic case being 87.7% of the Euler stress [10:08], [10:40]; Chapter H for combined axial-plus-moment beam-column design of singly symmetric reinforcement [08:05], [15:30]; Chapter C and Appendix 8 (B1 amplification factor) for second-order P-delta effects [11:11], [15:30]; Section F2 for doubly symmetric I-shapes, with Section F4 required for singly symmetric I-shapes (including web local buckling and the rule that J is taken as zero when the flange moment-of-inertia ratio is at or below 0.23) [58:16], [62:22], equation F4-2 referenced for the preload modification [78:23]; and the AISC Code of Standard Practice straightness tolerance of L over 1000 [09:07], [14:29]. He also referenced the Blodgett weld-distortion equation and an AISC Engineering Journal step-column paper (over 40 years old) plus the 1989 green-book ASD manual for non-stabilizing reinforcement [16:31], [27:23], [36:09]. No AISC Design Guide number, AISC 341 seismic section, AWS D1.1 clause, or RCSC bolting provision was cited; this presentation is member design, not connection or bolting design.

### Technical Details
The column design procedure runs seven steps: pick reinforcement type, estimate size, compute reinforced-section properties, assess geometric imperfections on flexural buckling, assess preload effect, assess partial-length reinforcement effect, then check strength per E3 [03:34], [07:05]. Doubly symmetric reinforcement is preferred because singly symmetric reinforcement shifts the centroid toward the added plate, creating an eccentric moment, and bows the member away from the weld [07:35], [08:05]. Total out-of-straightness delta-sub-D after welding sums rolling tolerance, P-delta, and weld distortion; if it exceeds the L/1000 tolerance delta-sub-O, the member is checked as a beam-column with a first-order moment of P-sub-R times (delta-D minus delta-O), amplified by B1 [11:44], [15:00]. Partial-length reinforcement (plates pulled back from member ends) is modeled as a step column using tabulated effective-length factors K-sub-S1 and K-sub-S2 (both greater than one, applied to reinforced-section properties), entered with the reinforced-to-original moment-of-inertia ratio and the length ratio [26:52], [29:27]. The worked example (W14x90, 18 ft, Fy 50 ksi, 300 kip preload, 1100 kip total factored LRFD load, 3/4 by 12 plates pulled back 4 ft each end) yielded K = 1.04, design strength 1500 kips against 1100 applied, and the bare W14x90 yield check of 1190 kips confirmed the end "ear" plates were unnecessary [39:53], [45:11]. For beams the controlling check is lateral torsional buckling per F4, with a step-beam C-prime-sub-B reduction factor beta-sub-LTB for partial-length reinforcement based on the setback-to-length ratio D-sub-R over L [70:40], [71:41].

### Fabrication Notes
Weld distortion scales with the square of the fillet leg size per Blodgett, so a 3/16 fillet may hold a member within L/1000 while doubling the leg roughly quadruples distortion to about a half inch, outside tolerance [18:04], [18:34]. Stitch welding reduces distortion, speeds the job, and lowers cost, with a reduction factor R-sub-S equal to stitch length over center-to-center spacing [19:11], [20:12]. Welding only one side of a partial-length plate kicks the column sideways, so symmetric welds and sequences that minimize distortion are specified by drawing note [22:16], [52:27]. Welding heat is a fabrication hazard on loaded members: a transverse weld can render an entire bottom flange ineffective, while a longitudinal weld removes much less, handled by an effective-width method that deletes the heat-affected strip from the section [55:31], [82:33]. A bottom-flange plate is the speaker's preferred reinforcement because it clamps in place for easy fit-up and welds downhand, whereas a WT welded overhead runs about four times the cost of a downhand weld [52:27], [54:31].

### Erection Notes
The webinar centers on shop and design work, with limited field content. The key field point is that field welders cannot remove all preload before reinforcing an in-service member: industrial and power-plant columns may carry 60 to 100 kips of preload during welding, which is the entire reason the preload and stabilizing-reinforcement checks exist [06:35], [38:11], [48:18]. Doubly symmetric reinforcement gives field welders a chance to balance shrinkage from both flanges and keep the post-welded member within the L/1000 straightness tolerance [08:36], [22:16]. No crane, rigging, sequencing, or shoring procedures were discussed.

### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
The dominant defect is excess weld distortion pushing the post-welded member outside the Code of Standard Practice L/1000 straightness limit, driven by oversized fillets, one-sided welding, or singly symmetric layouts [18:34], [22:16]. The speaker flagged that upward camber from a bottom-flange weld can crack an attached floor slab if not anticipated [51:26], and that welding heat on a loaded member can reduce flange strength enough to fail a beam or column [55:31], [82:33]. He warned of an unconservative calculation trap: when computing the M-sub-BR ratio for the step-beam factor, the actual J must be used rather than J equal to zero, or beta-LTB comes out too large [73:43]. No third-party inspection, NDE, or rejection categories were named.

### Bid / Cost Implications
Cost drivers named explicitly: weld position (overhead WT reinforcement runs about four times the cost of a downhand bottom-flange plate) [54:31]; weld volume, since stitch welds cut welding time, cost, and distortion versus continuous welds [19:11]; and matching reinforcement plates left and right to reuse one plate and one weld detail rather than fabricating odd tab or wing plates [23:47], [25:51]. The worked example shows good engineering deletes unneeded steel: the end ear plates were dropped after the bare-column yield check passed, removing distortion, cost, and schedule [45:43]. The trade-off between unloading a column (a months-long plant shutdown that may cost more than building a new unit) and designing for preload is itself a major schedule and cost decision [06:35].
