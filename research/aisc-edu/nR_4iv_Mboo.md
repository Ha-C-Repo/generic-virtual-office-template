### Core Concept
Rafael Sabelli (Walter P Moore) teaches Session 2 of the AISC Night School seismic course, covering special and intermediate steel moment frames. The controlling idea is capacity design: the beam flexural plastic hinge is the intended fuse, so the frame is proportioned for flexural yielding to form in the beams at each end while columns, panel zones, and connections stay essentially elastic [10:54], [16:41]. This prevents soft-story mechanisms and avoids yielding the high-axial-load columns. The session walks the portal-frame sub-assemblage from inflection point to inflection point as the basic unit of the frame, derives beam shear from the probable plastic moment, then derives column moment, column shear, and panel zone shear from that beam capacity [29:06], [30:09].

### Spec References
Speaker-cited: AISC 341 (Seismic Provisions), with explicit mention of the 2010 vs 2016 editions for the probable-moment strain-hardening factor and the strong-column-weak-beam ratio in "Chapter E" (M*pc/M*pb) [37:36], [86:33], [88:05]. AISC 358 the Prequalified Connection Standard, which sets the 1.15 strain-hardening factor adopted in 2016 (the 2010 provisions used 1.1) [38:10], [77:00]. AISC 360 for panel zone shear strength and concentrated-force limit states [61:33]. AWS D1.1 for welding (cited generically as the welding code violated in pre-Northridge practice) [51:33] and AWS D1.8 (Seismic Supplement) for weld dams not allowed, the large clip dimensions on continuity plates, and demand-critical weld toughness [53:39], [60:59]. The probable beam moment is Mpr = 1.1 (2010) or 1.15 (2016) x Ry x Fy x Z, with Ry x Fy = 55 ksi for A992 [37:36]. The 2016 PJP column-splice rule requires at least 85 percent effective throat [57:48].

### Technical Details
Connections discussed: reduced beam section (RBS, four bites out of the flanges to make a weak zone away from the column face) [13:31], [67:48]; welded unreinforced flange welded web (WUF-W) [55:43]; bolted flange plate and bolted end plate [68:19]; proprietary SidePlate, Kaiser bolted bracket, ConXtech ConXL two-way connection on a concrete-filled box column, Simpson Strong Frame, and the TT connection (2016 additions) [69:20], [69:53], [70:27]. Key geometry symbols: Mpr probable moment at hinge, Sh offset (about half beam depth from column face), Lh hinge-to-hinge length, Mcl moment at column centerline [34:25], [37:04]. Strong-column-weak-beam ratio greater than 1.0 addresses story mechanisms; a ratio well above 2.0 is needed to actually prevent column flexural yielding [78:07]. Panel zone shear equals the full change of column shear across the beam depth, reduced by the story column shear [43:46]. M*pc accounts for axial demand: ZcFy minus the axial term using the overstrength load combination [87:33].

### Fabrication Notes
The beam-flange-to-column-flange CJP groove weld is the critical fabrication detail. Modern practice CJP-welds the beam web to the column flange rather than bolting it, so the web participates and unloads the flanges [54:40], [64:37]. Backing bars (3/8 inch steel) are tacked, and tacks are incorporated into the final weld; weld tabs create run-off so starts and stops fall outside the joint [65:11]. At the bottom flange, current detailing removes the backing, back-gouges, reweld with a reinforcing fillet, and grinds smooth to remove the unfused notch that acted like a built-in crack starter [52:37], [55:43], [65:44]. Weld access holes are enlarged to relieve restraint and spread strain. Higher-toughness weld filler is mandatory at demand-critical welds. Continuity plates need large clips per AWS D1.8 to keep welding on the flat web [60:59]. Ultrasonic testing inspects the finished CJP for inclusions and discontinuities [66:15].

### Erection Notes
Stability bracing is required at the column and near (not within) the plastic hinge location, plus bracing at a defined spacing along the beam [71:00]. An adequate composite deck can replace the brace adjacent to the plastic hinge per the seismic provisions [71:34]. Protected zones, roughly one beam depth in from the column face, prohibit field attachments such as shot pins, low-toughness tack welds, deck puddle welds, and similar in the hinge region [72:06]. Column splices and column bases are also demand-critical weld locations and need that toughness and detailing in the field [72:39]. The speaker did not discuss crane, rigging, or lift sequencing.

### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
The Northridge lessons are the core failure catalog: bottom-flange CJP weld fracture and fracture into adjacent base metal [48:57]. Causes named: high strain at the column face with no demand relief, bolted (slipping) webs that forced the flanges to carry web moment and shear, weld access holes that failed to relieve restraint, column flange bending without continuity plates, low-toughness weld filler, beam material that ran grade 50 instead of the assumed grade 36 (so strong-column-weak-beam math worked only on paper), unfused steel backing acting as a 3/8 inch built-in crack, weld dams not allowed by AWS D1.8, too few weld passes, and poor welding and inspection that let welders do things AWS D1.1 prohibited [49:27], [51:02], [53:09]. Bottom-flange welds are most defect-prone because the welder reaches through the web access hole and must start and stop there [53:39].

### Bid / Cost Implications
Seismic moment frames carry real fabrication labor and material cost beyond gravity framing. Cost drivers a takeoff must price: demand-critical high-toughness weld filler, backing removal plus back-gouge plus reweld plus grind at every bottom flange, enlarged weld access holes, CJP web welds, continuity plates and doubler plates where local limit states come up short, and full UT inspection of every moment connection [60:29], [66:15]. The session shows a redesign trade where a heavier column with a lighter beam keeps the same sub-assembly stiffness but can eliminate continuity and doubler plates [63:06], a tonnage-versus-shop-labor trade worth flagging on a bid. Prequalified RBS or WUF-W connections per AISC 358 avoid the cost and schedule hit of project-specific qualification testing.
