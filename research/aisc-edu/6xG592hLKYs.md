## NS24: Structural Stability - From Behavior to Practice, Session 1: Behavior of Compression Members

### Core Concept
This is a behavior-and-theory lecture on why steel compression members buckle and how the AISC column curve is built up from Euler's 1750 solution by removing his idealizing assumptions one at a time [17:20]. Ron Ziemann (Bucknell, AISC Committee on Specifications, chairs TC3 Loads Analysis and Stability) walks from full yielding through Euler elastic buckling to the real-world inelastic column, adding initial out-of-straightness, non-concentric load eccentricity, partial yielding, residual stresses, and end restraint [78:48]. The unifying message is that steel is slender because it has excellent strength-to-weight and stiffness-to-weight but must be priced competitively, so slender members in compression have a real possibility of buckling and must be designed for stability [04:40]. The distinction drawn is bifurcation theory (instantaneous, binary buckled or not) versus instability theory (a perfect-real column with imperfection that gradually loses stiffness and grows deflection until it cannot reach equilibrium) [06:42][07:44].

### Technical Details
Euler's assumptions are stated explicitly: prismatic member, fixed moment of inertia, small deflections, no bending prior to bifurcation, perfectly straight, concentrically loaded, elastic material with constant E, and pin-roller boundary conditions [40:10][41:22]. The buckling limit states for compression members are full yielding (squash load Py = Fy times A), flexural buckling (the focus), torsional buckling (cruciform sections), flexural-torsional buckling (singly symmetric sections where centroid and shear center differ), and local buckling of slender sections [12:15][15:49]. The moment amplification factor 1/(1 - P/Pe) is derived as the exact amplifier on an initial sine-wave imperfection of amplitude L/1000 [37:16][37:46]. Residual stresses from differential cooling of rolled shapes run 30 to 50 percent of yield, are largely independent of yield strength, and trigger early partial yielding; rotary straightening yields and unloads the section and relieves much of that residual stress [50:37][51:38][54:41]. The worked W14x145 example (Fy = 50 ksi, A = 42.7 in2, Py = 21.35) compared L/r of 50 and 150 across linear buckling, geometric nonlinear, material nonlinear, and full GMNIA analyses in MASTAN, landing the full inelastic strength near 40 ksi at L/r 50 and about 11 ksi at L/r 150 [53:33][60:50]. K factors derived: 0.7 for fixed-pinned, 0.5 for fixed-fixed (four times Euler strength), 1.0 for pin-pin, 2.0 for cantilever [68:30][76:41].

### Fabrication Notes
The fabrication-relevant content is about the rolled product, not shop work. Rolled wide-flange shapes cool non-uniformly off the mill, flange tips set first and lock in residual stress when the web-flange intersection later contracts, producing compression-tension self-equilibrating patterns [50:37][51:07]. Mills increasingly use near-net-section rolling, which reduces residual stress because less rolling is needed, and then rotary-straighten members that do not meet the Code of Standard Practice straightness tolerance [50:04][54:10]. The practical takeaway for a fabricator is that the member you receive is neither perfectly straight nor stress-free, and rotary-straightened material carries lower residual stress [51:38].

### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
No connection rejections or NDE failures are named since this lecture covers no connections. The conceptual error corrected by the poll is that residual stresses cause the bending in a compression member; they do not, they accentuate partial yielding, while bending comes from initial out-of-straightness and non-concentric axial load, the answer 83 percent of attendees got right [82:51][84:26]. A second cautioned error is over-trusting K-factor alignment charts and assuming a pin-roller idealization that almost never exists in a real frame [67:28][77:11]. The unconservatism question (is the single AISC column curve unconservative in some cases) was deferred to a later session [80:48].
