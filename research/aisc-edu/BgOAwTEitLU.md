## Night School 6, Session 5: Vertical Bracing Connections - Corner Connection Part 2

### Core Concept
William A. Thornton works a complete high-seismic corner bracing connection for a Special Concentrically Braced Frame (SCBF), an HSS brace into a single hinge plate (knife plate) that releases in-plane brace buckling rather than the usual out-of-plane two-T hinge zone [02:04][02:34][09:09]. The connection uses an extended shear plate to the column web and is solved by the Uniform Force Method Special Case 4, which models the column-web connection as if it were a column flange by taking the section out to the centroid of the bolt group [29:30][61:13]. The whole design chases four sub-connections (brace-to-gusset, gusset-to-column, gusset-to-beam, beam-to-column) and shows that nearly every limit state is loaded close to 1.0, unlike the non-seismic case in the prior session [07:46][60:08].

ASTM A325-N bolts and ASTM A500 HSS, all Grade 50 plate [07:15][07:46]. RCSC is named as a committee Thornton serves on but no pretensioning clause is cited [01:02].

### Technical Details
The brace is an HSS 5x5x3/8 (Fy 46, Ry 1.4) at 45 degrees, tensile demand 398 kips, compressive demand 312 kips at KL/r 63.1 [08:47][10:52]. The hinge plate is 1 in by 10 in Grade 50, sized for tension yield, checked for rupture (457 kips) across the 5/8 in slot, and confirmed against side-sway buckling at KL/r 12.5 using a hinge width of 3t per Taiwan and University of Washington research rather than the usual 2t [12:24][14:59][33:00]. The gusset settles at 1/2 in, sized by block shear and the Whitmore section (30-degree spread, length 21.8 in), which Thornton stresses is the main tool for checking gusset buckling along the line of action at K=0.5 [17:34][18:34][19:39]. The slotted HSS net area falls below gross, so it gets a pair of 3/8 in by 2-7/8 in reinforcing plates sized to stay inside the 3-5/16 in workable flat for fillet welding on the flat face [22:15][24:50].

### Fabrication Notes
The hinge plate is slotted to receive the gusset, not the reverse, because gusset stresses are less well known than the hinge plate [13:59]. Slot width is gusset thickness plus 1/8 in for assembly; the HSS net section subtracts the 1-1/8 in slot per wall [14:29][22:48]. Reinforcing-plate length is 6 in weld plus 6 in weld plus an erection clearance dimension X that the detailer cannot fully fix because it depends on how the erector inserts the brace [27:59][82:01]. In practice the reinforcing weld is run continuous, though technically you need 6 in of weld before the slot starts and 6 in after load transfers into the hinge plate [83:35][84:05]. Final fillet welds: gusset-to-beam 1/2 in for the first 6-3/4 in (one-sided because the beam flange is blocked and shipped flush), 1/4 in for the remaining 15 in, and a missing weld symbol on the reinforcing plates should read 1/4 in by 6 in [64:52][67:26][68:00].

### Erection Notes
Brace cut length is the work-point-to-work-point length (17 ft) minus twice the pull-back at each end (3 ft 7 in per end), giving an unbraced length of 118 in [09:18][09:49]. The erection clearance X in the slot exists only so the brace can be jockeyed into place after the beam and gusset above are set; Thornton has seen 1 in, 2 in, and up to 6 in used depending on the erector's sequence [82:33][83:03]. The beam ships flush at the cope, which limits the gusset-to-beam weld to one side over part of its length [65:22].

### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
Historically, gussets that ran the brace connection far out on unsupported plate buckled along the line of action; roofs came down and crews had to jack the structure and add reinforcing plates, which is exactly the failure the Whitmore buckling check now prevents [21:11][21:42]. A biaxial block-shear interaction check on the shear plate first came out at 1.14, over by 14 percent, forcing a move from a 1/2 in to a 5/8 in and ultimately 3/4 in plate [46:42][58:01]. Thornton flags that slotting the HSS directly to the gusset with no hinge line is a design error unless you then design for the full flexural strength of the brace, 1.1 Ry Fy Z [73:41][74:13]. A reviewer question confirms the slot reinforcement is mandatory because the provisions require effective net area to exceed gross, even though one researcher reported never seeing a slot failure in testing [80:28][81:28].

### Bid / Cost Implications
Thornton notes the connection is compact for shipping (about 4 ft from beam bottom flange to plate tip) even with 18 in of plate sticking out, so it is well within shipping limits [70:02][70:32]. He repeatedly points out where you could sharpen the pencil: the shear plate could drop from 3/4 in to 5/8 in if you do not assume worst-case biaxial loading, and the 18 in of 1/4 in fillet could shorten to 10 to 12 in with larger welds, though the 10-bolt count fixes the minimum plate length either way [58:01][70:32][70:50]. He distinguishes the conservative one-off hand calc (cover every worst case) from a vetted computer program that could refine each load case, accepting deliberate overkill to guarantee coverage on a manual design [53:25][53:56].

### Your Company Application
For Ivan's takeoff, this is the seismic premium made concrete: an HSS brace connection in an SCBF carries a 1 in hinge plate, two slot-reinforcement plates, a heavy 3/4 in extended shear plate, and a 33 in deep bolt group with 10 to 11 bolts, far more steel and labor than the non-seismic double-clip connection. For Tekla detailing, the slot must be longer than the reinforcing plate by an erection X dimension, the hinge plate is slotted (never the gusset), and reinforcing-plate width must respect the HSS workable flat so Mario's crew fillet-welds on the flat, not the radius [24:50][82:01]. For Mario's shop, welds are A325-N bolting plus 1/4 in and 1/2 in fillets with minimum fillet governed by the thinner of gusset or HSS wall; the beam ships flush at the cope, so plan one-sided access there [64:52][71:34]. For the in-house PE stamp, the controlling references to capture in a calc package are AISC 341-10 F2.6a demand equations, Manual Part 10 ductility (plate yields before bolt/weld fracture, 0.03 rad), and the Whitmore buckling check; note that Uniform Force Method Special Case 4 is not in the AISC Manual and rests on the lower-bound theorem, so a reviewer may question it [29:30][77:24].
