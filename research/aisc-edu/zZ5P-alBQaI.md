## Introduction to the  Steel Construction Process: The Team Behind the Building

### Core Concept
This is session one of AISC Night School 18, presented by Drew Toric of Ruben Associates, an overview of the entire steel construction team from the mill through topping out, organized around the AISC Code of Standard Practice (AISC 303) and who is responsible for what [07:11]. The controlling idea is that every party (owner, EOR, CM, mill, fabricator, detailer, connection designer, erector, erection engineer, QC/QA) has defined responsibilities, and understanding them all is what produces constructability [12:56]. Constructability is defined as the optimum use of construction knowledge in planning, procurement, and field operations, applied throughout design, fabrication, and erection, not as a one-time value-engineering or review session [11:17][12:23].
### Spec References
AISC 303 Code of Standard Practice (the governing document, 2016 edition) with specific sections cited: 2.1 (definition of structural steel) [07:41], 2.2 (what is not structural steel) [08:42], 6.4 (fabrication tolerances) [55:32], Section 7 erection and 7.5.1 (anchor rod setting tolerances) [57:07], and commentary to 7.10.1 (lateral force resisting system statement) [17:35]. AISC 360 Specification Chapter N (QC, QA, and NDT, new in 2010) [71:15]; AISC 341 Seismic Provisions (designation of seismic force resisting system, protected zones, notch toughness) [16:02]. ASCE 37 design loads on structures during construction [64:02]. Material/product specs cited verbatim: ASTM A992, A500 grade C and grade B, A1085, A36, A572 grade 50 [24:49 through 26:22], and ASTM F3125 (which superseded the withdrawn A325/A490, retaining grade A325 and grade A490; bolt Groups A/B/C at 120/150 and 200 ksi) [26:22 through 26:54]. Also AISC Design Guide on constructability, the column base plate and anchor rod and vertical bracing design guides, the clean columns spreadsheet [48:03], IBC special inspection tables, and Blodgett "Design of Welded Structures" [51:46].
### Technical Details
Structural steel per 2.1 is the frame elements shown and sized in the design documents to support design loads (beams, columns, bracing, leveling plates) [07:41]. Connection design has three Code options: option 1 fully shown on the design documents (common on the West Coast and in seismic regions), option 2 selected/completed by an experienced detailer from tables with EOR-provided loads, and option 3 delegated to a licensed engineer (added to the Code in 2010), split into 3A and 3B for member reinforcement [43:52 through 48:03]. Member reinforcement (web openings, bearing stiffeners, doublers) is explicitly NOT connection design and stays with the EOR [47:02]. The lateral force resisting system may combine steel and non-steel elements (metal deck, concrete diaphragm, masonry shear walls, precast, open web joist, cold-formed) and must be identified for construction-stage stability [17:04].
### Fabrication Notes
The fabricator turns stock into assemblies and sources steel plus buyout items like bolts [29:57]. Shop stations walked through: cut to length on a band saw, hole-making by multi-spindle drills (top flange, bottom flange, web at once), mag drill for field holes, punching plus angle shear on an "angle master," coping by manual plasma torch or a multi-axis robotic plasma coper that also burns holes, and plasma tables for plate stiffeners and gussets [30:57 through 34:03]. Layout/assembly joins pieces by bolt or weld; the common shop weld processes are FCAW and GMAW (both wire-fed), plus submerged arc welding (SAW) with a preheater and powdered flux on items like plate-girder web-to-flange [33:32 through 35:04]. Steel need not be painted if it will be enclosed, fireproofed, or encased in concrete [35:39]. Fabrication tolerances live in Code section 6.4 and are coordinated with the erection tolerances [55:32].
### Erection Notes
The erector owns the means, methods, and safety of erecting the steel frame [53:55]. Before the erector starts, the ODRC (GC/CM) must provide access, a dry flat site, laydown/shakeout storage, foundations, setting lines, benchmarks, and installed anchor bolts; grouting under base plates is a separate trade the ODRC coordinates [19:42 through 20:46]. Columns are set on shims or leveling nuts until connected and plumbed. Erection tolerances in Section 7 have been essentially unchanged since 1959 and are tied to the H/500 out-of-plumb limit that yields the 0.002 direct-analysis notional load coefficient; a column may land up to 3 in out / 2 in toward the building exterior at the 36th floor and above [55:32 through 57:07]. The erector furnishes and installs temporary supports (typically wire rope with turnbuckles) sufficient against erection loads but need not consider others' work loads or hurricanes; ASCE 37 governs reduced construction wind, the CF shape factor for square-sided members, and limited shielding (only a 20% reduction past the third beam in a row) [62:29 through 67:07].
### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
Anchor bolt placement tolerance is called out as a frequent field problem, drawing a laugh from the speaker's own office [58:07]. The Code treats moderate reaming, grinding, welding, cutting, and drift-pin drawing as normal erection operations; anything beyond that is a field error to be resolved [58:07]. Chapter N inspection tables flag tasks "observe" (random basis) versus "perform" (every welded joint or bolted connection), analogous to IBC periodic versus continuous; where both QC and QA letters match, coordinated inspection is allowed [73:50 through 74:56]. Bid-time risks named: unidentified member reinforcement quantities, blanket "design all connections for a percentage of UDL" notes (discouraged; provide actual member-end loads), and mismatched bolt groups (a Group A bolt forced into a Group B hole) [45:31][52:48].
### Bid / Cost Implications
The cost-to-influence curve is the central money lesson: the ability to influence cost is highest early and the cost of change grows exponentially once documents, fabrication, and erection begin [11:17]. Mill orders must be timed to the rolling schedule; once a mill order is released the EOR must lock member sizes and lengths because re-rolling can take months, and an accurate advance bill of materials is required [28:26 through 28:56]. A572 grade 50 plate often carries no surcharge over A36, giving 40% stronger connection material for free [25:51]. Member reinforcement is costly, so a heavier clean member can beat a reinforced one (the clean columns spreadsheet) [48:03]. Tight or constrained sites, owner-dictated erection sequences, hurricane-season bracing readiness, critical lifts (two-crane or over 75% capacity), and tighter-than-standard tolerances for AESS all add cost and must be priced and stated at bid time [42:50][56:03][67:38][80:33].
