### Technical Details
Weldability is governed by carbon content compounded by alloy, and is inversely related to hardenability; low-carbon low-alloy steels weld best, and sulfur and phosphorus drive hot cracking [06:46][08:17]. F1554 is the preferred anchor-rod spec, but grade 36 carries a manganese footnote, grade 55 has no carbon or alloy limits unless ordered to Supplement S1, and grade 105 (105 ksi yield) is essentially uncontrolled chemistry and a near-guaranteed weldability challenge [10:24][19:45]. Suppliers often substitute grade 55 for an ordered grade 36, so weldability must be verified case by case via a lab chemistry check and the carbon-equivalency equations on the mill test report [11:27][15:07]. Heavy/jumbo shapes (former group 4 and 5, example W14x730 with 5 in flange, 3 in web, 22 in depth) have a segregated higher-carbon, lower-toughness core that becomes problematic only in tension joints, which is why CVN is now sampled at the centerline t/4 location [44:55][53:33]. Fracture is controlled by three variables: toughness, residual or applied stress, and crack size, and triaxial tensile stress at a weld access hole removes the shear needed for ductility per the Mohr-circle argument from Gensamer (1941) [49:59][57:40][66:24].

### Fabrication Notes
For anchor-rod extensions, first check mechanical options (coupler nuts, undercutting and milling the base plate); if welding, use a weldable-steel doughnut washer with the ID matched to the rod OD as a shelf and runoff tab, grind the extension to a chisel point, weld one side, back-gouge the root, weld the second, then grind flush, and never use a plug weld (no tension capacity) [04:40][20:17][22:18]. On heavy sections, AISC mid-1980s rules require preheating to 150 F before thermal cutting the access hole, grinding to bright metal, then MT or PT inspection to start crack-free; the preferred practice drills the access-hole radius on the drill line to avoid the hard-to-grind radius notch [72:33][73:04]. Access holes are enlarged (height 1.5 t-sub-w, min 1 in, max 2 in; length on the W14x730 reaches about 15 in) specifically to keep flange-weld and web-weld residual stresses from interacting [69:27][70:29]. Reduce weld volume with a double-V groove, sequence so the final high-residual passes land away from the core, and use higher-toughness filler (20 ft-lb at minus 40 F for weld metal) [70:29][55:38].

### Erection Notes
The recurring field trigger is anchor rods set too deep (no thread engagement, rod below the base-plate plane) or too shallow, which tempts crews to field weld; the speaker's first answer is a mechanical fix and telling the rod-setting contractor not to set too deep [03:39][16:40][24:21]. The EDS/Plano suspended-truss case study shows the erection strategy for jumbo work: make the complicated nodes in the shop and leave simple butt splices for the field where the welder has better access away from the column, and leave the large access holes open after they have done their residual-stress job [75:40][77:42]. When welding on existing structures under load, place the work lead as close to the weld as possible so welding current does not stray through conduit, HVAC ducting, or gas lines [99:15][99:46].

### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
The Orlando Civic Center jumbo bottom-chord tension truss fractured in the base metal (not the weld) at the weld access hole during night erection in the late 1970s, traced to small access holes terminating in residual tension plus pre-existing PT-detectable notches and gouges [42:50][67:24][72:03]. Named failures include plug welds in tension on anchors (no capacity), tack welds on the inboard side of a rod-end nut (can crack the rod), incomplete fusion through a heavy galvanized coating so strength rides only on the zinc, severe porosity from zinc sublimation, and zinc-induced centerline cracking [22:18][23:50][36:41]. Inspecting welds after heavy paint or dipping can mask discontinuities, so visual inspection must occur before painting [34:36][35:08]. Lamellar tearing still occurs with large through-thickness CJP welds, most often at plate edges and fabricated-box corner joints [74:05].

### Bid / Cost Implications
Welding choices carry direct cost. Continuous AESS welds cost more than intermittent welds and add distortion, so caulk between welds can be cheaper where strength does not require a full weld [90:29]. AESS demands mockups painted in the final finish and viewed from the actual installed distance (a 30 ft-high truss judged from 30 ft), and the AISC AESS document supplies estimated premium costs by category for use with architects [88:55][93:35]. Critical coated-steel applications require WPS qualification by test rather than relying on prequalified procedures and mocking up the thickest coating condition, which adds qualification time and cost [39:46][40:17]. HSS connection detailing can force a member-size change, and overlapped HSS joints need multiple cuts (more fit-up risk) versus a single-cut gapped connection [80:47][84:20].
