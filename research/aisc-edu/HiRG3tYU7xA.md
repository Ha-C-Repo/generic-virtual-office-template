## AISC Live Webinar - Are You Properly Specifying Materials?
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HiRG3tYU7xA | Duration: 1:02:32 | Upload: NA | Tier: 1 | Watched: 2026-06-24
### Core Concept
Charlie Carter (AISC VP and Chief Structural Engineer) walks through how to specify the correct ASTM material standard and grade for every common structural steel product, so that what is drawn matches what is economically available in the marketplace [00:02]. The thesis is that calling out the right ASTM standard pulls in everything that makes the material what you expect: ordering requirements, mechanical properties (yield, tensile, elongation), chemistry, process, testing, cross-section tolerances, defect repair, and reporting [08:47]. The quick-reference source is the table in AISC Code of Standard Practice Section 6.1.1, which lists the preferred (most common) ASTM standard for each product, plus the more extensive material tables in Part 2 of the AISC Manual and the Modern Steel Construction article of the same name [10:19].

RCSC Section 2.4, Section 2.6.2, and Sections 7/8/9 (preinstallation verification, installation, inspection) at boltcouncil.org [42:18][86]. ### Technical Details
W shapes are almost all A992 (Fy 50 ksi); A913 is the high-strength alternative with size minimums for the quench-and-self-temper process (W14x90 and up, W12x65 and up, W10x49 and up), and grades 50 and 65 need no preheat while grade 70 still does [16:59][19:31][20:32]. S, M, C/MC, and L shapes sit in the 36-or-50 ksi overlap where the asterisk grade can be A572 Gr 50, A529 Gr 50, or A992 [21:33][38:08]. Rectangular and square HSS are A500 Gr C at 50 ksi yield, but round HSS at the same grade is only 46 ksi, and round HSS is not interchangeable with A53 Gr B pipe at 35 ksi [27:47][33:32]. A1085 holds Fy 50 ksi for both round and rectangular, has a tighter 5 percent wall tolerance that removes the 0.93 design-thickness reduction, plus a max tensile and specified toughness [31:27][56:13].

### Fabrication Notes
The Section 6.1.1 table exists to simplify shop and field material identification: preferred standard grades need no special marking, only off-grade material gets marked, so a fabricator should preserve that marking discipline [10:49]. The MC12x14.3 stringer channel has a 2-1/8 inch flange specifically to land a railing post and make the weld without crimping the pipe or building up weld over an overhang [23:38]. When the wall thickness governs design, A500 product is reduced to 0.93 of nominal, while A1085 and A1065 are used at full thickness [31:57][56:13]. A1085 toughness lets it be used in dynamically loaded welded structures, which plain A500 prohibits [32:31].

### Erection Notes
The headline erection-relevant item is anchor-rod fit at column bases [46:26]. Mike West coordinated ACI 117 anchor placement tolerances, the Code of Standard Practice anchor-rod tolerances, and the 15th edition AISC Manual hole sizes so that rods set by the concrete contractor to ACI 117 receive a base plate fabricated to Code of Standard Practice tolerances using Manual hole sizes, aimed at ending base-fit problems [46:26]. No crane, rigging, or sequencing content beyond base-plate fit is covered.

### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
The single most common mis-specification named is calling out "A325 anchor bolts," because anchor rods are not bolts; the supplier then quietly furnishes A449 rod cut to length and the buyer believes they got A325 [61:18]. Specifying A513 tube (common in handrails) is a defect because A513 carries no minimum strength level, so yard stock was never ordered to a strength [37:06]. Over-specifying bolts with N, X, or SC suffixes in the purchase order is unnecessary since those are design/installation conditions, not the fastener ordered [57:44]. Stale weld references like AWS A5.1/A5.5 are often wrong because they name stick welding, not the shop processes actually used [48:31]. Welding high-strength heat-treated bolts is cautioned against [59:18].

### Bid / Cost Implications
Specifying the preferred, most-available grade avoids time chasing scarce or pricier alternatives, so correct specification is also the fast and economical path [50:33]. Carter declines to state prices and repeatedly directs the estimator to producer cost sheets and AISC member service centers for live pricing, noting the upcharge for higher-strength steel often does not keep pace with the strength gain, meaning you pay comparatively less per unit of strength [53:07][60:18]. Alternate grades (lighter table shading) should be priced and confirmed with a fabricator or supplier for cost and availability before being assumed [13:52]. A500 Gr B versus Gr C carries no exhaustion lag because mills dual-label and service centers stock the dual product, so the higher strength is free to take [51:35].
