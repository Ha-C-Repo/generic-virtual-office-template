## It Doesn't Get Built Without the Erector
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wou7aR18MR4 | Duration: 1:32:11 | Upload: NA | Tier: 1 | Watched: 2026-06-24
### Core Concept
This is the erector's view of the steel project, presented by Phil Torchio (retired president of Williams Enterprises) in AISC Night School Session 5. It walks the full erector lifecycle: how erectors get work from fabricators, how an erection estimate is built piece by piece off the drawings, how the AISC Code of Standard Practice (COSP) allocates responsibility, how crane and hoisting capacity drives the whole plan, and how the field actually sets and welds the steel. The recurring theme is that the erector sells labor and hoisting, prices in pieces and crane picks (not tons), and that detail geometry and weld type are the real cost drivers, not headline tonnage [08:24][09:50].
### Spec References
AISC Code of Standard Practice, Section 7 Erection, cited section by section on a slide: 7.1 Method of Erection, 7.2 Job-Site Conditions, 7.3 Foundations Piers and Abutments, 7.4 Lines and Benchmarks, 7.5 Installation of Anchor Rods Foundation Bolts and Other Embedded Items, 7.6 Installation of Bearing Devices, 7.7 Grouting, 7.8 Field Connection Material, 7.9 Loose Material, 7.10 Temporary Support of Structural Steel Frames (ODRD), 7.11 Safety Protection, 7.12 Structural Steel Frame Tolerances, 7.13 Erection Tolerances, 7.14 Correction of Errors, 7.15 Cuts Alterations and Holes for Other Trades, 7.16 Handling and Storage, 7.17 Field Painting, 7.18 Final Cleaning Up [14:57][15:55]. AWS D1.1 named, with AWS D1.8 cited as the supplemental seismic welding standard requiring qualified WPS and a separate welder qualification for moment connections [21:05][21:48]. AWS A2.4 cited for the 2020 change to the PJP weld symbol convention from S(E)/D(S) to D(S) where D is the depth of groove and S is the size of weld [50:54][51:24]. The AESS (Architecturally Exposed Structural Steel) category matrix from COSP is discussed by category, AESS 1 through 4 plus Category C custom [18:37].
### Technical Details
The reference building is a 26-story rectangular braced-frame office tower in downtown Charlotte (300 Tryon), chosen as a clean erector case. The lateral system is a stiff braced core: vertical X and tubular K bracing in the core, so once the core is plumb the building is plumb and few stability cables are needed [28:36][29:36]. Bottom-shaft built-up columns are W14x1400 at Fy=65 ksi, a 30-foot starter shaft weighing about 42,000 pounds, with diagonals marked W14x145 carrying P=790k to 850k T or C from the design drawings [28:36, frame slide 39/45]. The frame moment column splice detail calls a CJP single-bevel groove for flanges and web, prepped with a 30-degree bevel and 3/8 inch root, the most efficient field joint prequalified in D1.1 [48:12]. COSP plumbness and erection tolerances are illustrated by the standard nomograph, with the caveat that intentionally non-plumb columns (Football Hall of Fame example) require the contract to define work points explicitly [17:54]. The AESS matrix scales finish quality by sight distance, AESS 2 beyond 20 feet, AESS 3 within 20 feet, AESS 4 touchable by the public; Category C is a pure custom architect direction that does not inherit the other AESS elements and requires a visual sample [18:37].
### Fabrication Notes
The erector relies on the fabricator to detail for field constructibility: lifting holes in columns to pin the column hitch, drilled holes in beams for catenary fall-protection lines and safety cable, cable posts for interior openings, and bolted rather than welded field connections where the erector needs to plumb before final weld (parapet kicker diagonals, K-brace tubular connections wanted as 2-bolt so a diagonal stays put) [10:50][27:37][29:48]. Shop assembly of small trusses and roof frames is agreed at the pre-detailing meeting under the contract clause that assemblies be shop-done where practical; large trusses cannot ship assembled [90:06]. For framing into concrete the erector wants the fabricator to supply a welded angle seat or welded shear tab with slotted holes because embeds and concrete are never exactly placed, and elevator-separator beams cut slightly short [25:46][51:58]. Bent plate is shipped loose (welded-on plate stops beams nesting on the truck and the bent-plate edge must stay straight for curtain wall) [80:54]. The galvanized 1/64-inch-tolerance wind-turbine embed shows fabrication QC far tighter than AISC standard, with the fabricator engineer computing both dead-load deflection and concrete float of the embed during placement [58:38].
### Erection Notes
Hoisting governs the plan. On 300 Tryon the GC furnished tower cranes (Peiner SK 415, L4 boom); at the 133-foot lift radius crane capacity is 22,025 lb, limited not by radius but by an 11,000-lb two-part hoist, and an imposed 85 percent safety factor drops allowable to 18,720 lb, which would cap a W14x1400 pick at only 13 feet [30:55][frame slide 45]. The fix: bring in a 275-ton hydro to set the first-tier full columns, then cut upper columns in half and reeve four parts of line on the tower crane, gaining three floors of work while splices are welded to hold schedule [32:50]. Steel is specified delivered to the hook; the erector unloads, checks webs vertical, shakes out on dunnage in a graveled lay-down area, then Christmas-trees up to five beams on a bull tail with a multiple-lift procedure [82:11][85:55]. Sequence plan is set jointly by erector and fabricator, sized by lay-down area and site access (Charlotte allowing trucks only 6 to 7 am), and is generally not stamped unless erecting over occupied space (the McLeod hospital over-build had the whole erection plan stamped) [84:48][86:37]. Marshaling steel to a remote yard is avoided as pure added cost [84:55].
### Common Errors / Inspection Failures
The detail-review slides flag the bad-vs-good edge-of-slab condition (A3 vs A4 on S300): the rejectable detail buries an intermittent 3/16 fillet 2-inch-on-12 weld under the bent plate where the welder cannot reach without angel-wing or rolling scaffold, so a plug or slot weld through the bent plate or a bolted bent-plate support angle with slots is preferred [24:06][frame slide 33]. Deformed bar extending over the top flange is called a tripping hazard and a reason to keep the bent plate off the beam [25:46]. Loose stud plates that sink in the pour should be tack-welded to hold elevation for precast [26:10]. CJP column splices receive ultrasonic testing, and the speaker notes the allowable for a PJP is intentionally lower than a CJP because the code anticipates a welder takes extra care on a joint he knows will be UT'd [52:02]. Flange misalignment at the splice is corrected with a 150-ton porta-power between the flanges to seat a tight backing bar before welding [49:58]. Silica dust is the current exposure concern, mitigated with HEPA drills on concrete connections [37:17].
### Bid / Cost Implications
The estimate is built in pieces and crane picks, not tons; GCs and fabricators convert erection price to dollars-per-ton only as a negotiating metric [09:50]. The headline cost lesson is the CJP-to-PJP conversion on the W14x1400 splices. A full-capacity CJP flange weld holds about 498 cubic inches of weld plus 108 root and 83 in runoff tabs, roughly 193 lb of weld needing about 230 lb of flux-core wire; at 35 lb deposited per shift that is 6.5 man-days per splice. Converting to PJP sized to the actual design tension plus a 200 kip-ft erection load drops it to about 95 lb of flux core at 50 lb deposited per day, 1.9 man-days, saving about 1,800 dollars in direct cost per joint plus large schedule savings [48:52][52:02]. Pricing builds true direct cost first, then a markup (you can mess with markup, never direct cost), then base sales price plus alternates [37:42][frame slide 62]. Contract risk is real: flow-down clauses, pay-when-paid as a condition precedent, requirement-to-proceed-without-change-orders, and liquidated damages the erector tries to cap near 1 percent of contract price [42:01][44:01][frame slide 66]. Weather show-up pay, 440 three-phase power, builders-risk deductibles, and CCIP/OCIP terms are all priced in [37:42][frame slide 17].
### Your Company Application
This video is a direct playbook for Your Company as a combined fabricator-erector. For Ivan's takeoff, mirror the estimate slide: count columns/beams/girders/joists, stud count, vertical X-or-K and horizontal bracing, deck SF, deck edge angle and bent plate, then compute moment-connection and column-splice weld weight as explicit cost drivers, run through aisc_validator.py for member weights rather than trusting LLM math, and price in crane picks not tons. The CJP-to-PJP conversion is a concrete value-engineering lever Mario and the PE stamp can apply: when a splice CJP is sized for full member capacity but the actual demand plus an erection allowance is lower, an in-house PE can stamp a PJP that cuts weld volume and man-days dramatically, with the AWS A2.4 2020 D(S) symbol convention noted for detailing. Mario's AWS D1.1 work gains the D1.8 supplemental note: seismic moment connections need qualified WPS and a separate welder qualification, so screen ACP and any seismic job for that requirement.
