### Core Concept
The speaker, Erica Fisher of Oregon State University, introduces a set of AISC educational teaching modules on structural fire engineering, defined as the design of structural components and frames to maintain load-carrying capacity throughout a design-basis fire [00:01]. The controlling goal is that components deform in a way that preserves compartmentalization, which stops fire from spreading horizontally across a floor plate or vertically through the building [00:31]. The talk is a curriculum overview aimed at university instructors, not a fabrication or erection webinar, and it notes this topic is absent from most undergraduate civil engineering programs including the prescriptive approach [00:31].

### Technical Details
The four modules build sequentially: (1) a strength-of-materials module where students test tension coupons at elevated temperatures, plot stress-strain curves, and calculate Young's modulus and yield stress, comparing to the AISC Appendix 4 published values [05:07] [05:38]; (2) a compression-capacity module computing a column's capacity at a fixed elevated temperature or across a full fire as temperatures change [06:10]; (3) a system-level module examining how forces in columns and beams shift as the design fire changes and the resulting member force demands [06:42] [07:12]; and (4) a prescriptive fire-protection-design module covering the types of fire protection and how they work [07:12]. The pedagogy is built around the spacing effect, distributing the material across the undergraduate sequence rather than in one dedicated course [02:05] [02:35].
